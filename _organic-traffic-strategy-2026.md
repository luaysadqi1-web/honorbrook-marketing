## 3. Content Strategy (building on the existing blog + local-page engine)

**What's already working structurally:** BlogPosting + FAQPage schema, 800–1100 word posts, internal linking, author schema with NPN. Keep that format — it's aligned with what's recommended for YMYL content in 2026 (see next section). The strategic question now is *what* to write, not *how*.

### 3a. Ride the AEP calendar aggressively — you're in the highest-intent window right now
Today is Aug 4, 2026. The 2026 Annual Enrollment Period (for 2027 coverage) runs **Oct 15 – Dec 7, 2026** — roughly 10 weeks out. Search volume for AEP-adjacent terms ramps hard starting late September. (medicareresources.org / AHA News coverage of 2026 AEP dates, current as of research date) You already have `/blog/aep-2026-whats-changing` — plan a companion piece now that will need refreshing to "AEP 2027" language and 2027 plan/premium figures once CMS releases them (typically late September/early October). Also worth a dedicated **"What Changes for [Northern Virginia city] Medicare Members in 2027"**-style post that links out to your existing city pages — this is a natural bridge between the blog engine and the local-page engine you already have.

Also don't neglect the secondary window: **Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA-OEP), Jan 1 – Mar 31** — a real but under-content-targeted second wave where people who picked wrong in AEP can switch once. A post like "Made the Wrong Medicare Advantage Choice? You Have Until March 31" performs well because competitors mostly stop publishing after AEP ends.

### 3b. Mine "People Also Ask" and AI-answer-box questions directly into FAQ schema
Your product pages already carry FAQPage schema — keep feeding it with real PAA-sourced questions rather than invented ones (PAA questions are literally what Google has already validated as real search intent). For Medicare topics specifically, PAA clusters tend to run around: cost comparisons ("how much does Medigap Plan G cost in Virginia"), eligibility edge cases ("can I switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare"), and timing ("what happens if I miss AEP"). Your existing posts (Medicare Advantage vs Medigap, Part D costs, AEP changes) already hit the big three — the next tier of content should go one level more specific: plan-type comparisons (you already added `medigap-plan-g-vs-plan-n.html` — good instinct, keep doing this for Plan N vs Plan G vs Plan G HD, MA-PPO vs MA-HMO, etc.) and locality-specific cost questions ("what does Medicare Advantage cost in Fairfax County vs. rural Virginia").

### 3c. Programmatic local + state pages: you're currently on the safe side of the doorway-page line, but watch page depth
Google's 2026 guidance is explicit that the penalty trigger isn't "programmatically generated," it's **"provides no genuine value a user in this location couldn't find on any other page."** (guptadeepak.com, "The Programmatic SEO Paradox," 2026; Search Foundry, "Programmatic SEO on a Budget," 2026) Recommended minimum bar: unique H1/meta per page (you have this), 300+ words of genuinely local content, references to local landmarks/neighborhoods, and — if not already present — an embedded Google Map and LocalBusiness schema per city page (verify this exists on `/vienna-medicare-insurance.html` etc.; the homepage has full LocalBusiness schema, but confirm the city pages either inherit it correctly via canonical/shared markup or carry their own). As you expand to more Northern Virginia towns or additional licensed states, keep each page's content meaningfully distinct — a plan-name/city mail-merge without local specifics (average premiums in that county, nearby hospital network notes, local Medicare/SHIP office info) is exactly what 2026 guidance flags as thin doorway content.

### 3d. Video is a real gap
You don't have YouTube presence noted anywhere in the site (open question, but nothing links to a channel). Even 2–3 minute videos — "What is Medicare Advantage in plain English," "AEP 2026 explained," a filmed version of your About page story — get indexed separately by Google (video rich results) and are increasingly cited as sources in AI Overviews and Perplexity answers, which favor multi-format sourcing. Low cost relative to payoff given you already have the founder on camera in photos.

## 4. Technical / Structural

### 4a. JobPosting schema fixes for /careers.html
Current state (`/Users/luaysadqi/honorbrook-marketing/site/careers.html`): 5 listings, each with `title`, `description`, `datePosted`, `validThrough`, `employmentType`, `hiringOrganization`, `jobLocation`, `directApply`, `url`. That covers Google's *required* fields. Gaps against Google's *recommended* fields that affect actual visibility in Google for Jobs results:
- **`baseSalary` is missing on all 5.** Google increasingly favors postings with salary data in the Jobs surface, and several job boards now require it by policy; even a range (e.g., `"baseSalary":{"@type":"MonetaryAmount","currency":"USD","value":{"@type":"QuantitativeValue","minValue":X,"maxValue":Y,"unitText":"YEAR"}}`) helps. For commission-based agent roles, Google does accept a stated range or a `unitText` of "COMMISSION" style description in `description` text, but structured `baseSalary` is still the stronger signal if you're willing to publish a range.
- **`identifier` is missing** — a unique job ID per posting (helps Google dedupe and track the listing across updates without treating an edit as a brand-new posting).
- **Freshness risk:** all 5 listings share `validThrough: 2026-11-30` set from `datePosted: 2026-08-04`. Google's stated behavior is that stale/expired JobPosting markup left live on a domain degrades that domain's overall "freshness" trust for Google for Jobs over time — "repeated expired listings lead to Google reducing your domain's Google Jobs trust score." (schemavalidator.org, "JobPosting JSON-LD: Required Fields for Google for Jobs," 2026) Action: put a recurring reminder ~2 weeks before Nov 30, 2026 to either update `datePosted`/`validThrough` for roles still open, or set `"eventStatus":"https://schema.org/EventCancelled"` (or remove the block entirely) for roles that are filled. Don't let closed listings sit live with valid-looking schema.
- Minor: Google's guidance says the `title` field should contain *only* the job title — no company name, location, or extra qualifiers. Yours currently pass this test (e.g., "Licensed Health Insurance Agent (Medicare Specialist)" — the parenthetical specialization is fine, it's not a location/company/date, so this is compliant, just flagging the rule since it's the single most commonly-violated field per research). (Cavuno, "Job Posting Schema," 2026)
- Confirm only one canonical page carries this markup (yours is one `/careers` page for all 5 — that's Google's recommended structure, "one job per page" is only a *preference* when you have dedicated per-role landing pages; a single multi-posting careers page is an accepted pattern too as long as each `JobPosting` block is complete and distinguishable).

### 4b. Core Web Vitals
No live measurement was available to me (would need PageSpeed Insights / Search Console access — see Open Questions), but worth knowing the 2026 baseline: **INP (Interaction to Next Paint) replaced FID as a ranking-relevant metric in March 2024 and is now fully embedded**; "good" thresholds are LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1, and Google treats CWV as roughly a 10–15% tiebreaker factor, not a dominant ranking signal — it matters most when competing against similarly-optimized competitor content. (corewebvitals.io, 2026; ALM Corp, "Core Web Vitals 2026: Technical SEO That Actually Moves the Needle," 2026) Given the site is static HTML/CSS with no heavy JS framework, you're structurally well-positioned to pass CWV easily — the main risks for a site like this are usually unoptimized images (check `luay-sadqi.jpg`, `og-image.jpg`, and the `logo-animated.gif` in `/assets/` for file size — animated GIFs in particular can be surprisingly heavy vs. a modern video/WebP equivalent) and third-party script blocking (the chatbot widget `chat.js` and GA4 `gtag.js` are the two scripts most likely to affect INP if not loaded with `defer`/`async`). Recommend running PageSpeed Insights on the homepage, one product page, and one blog post as a baseline once Search Console access is sorted.

## 6. AI Search / 2026 Shifts (AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity)

This is the newest and least mature terrain, but the direction is now measurable:

- **AI answer engines are far more selective than classic search.** One 2026 industry index found AI platforms recommend only about **1.2% of local businesses in ChatGPT answers and 7.4% in Perplexity**, versus roughly 36% visibility in Google's local 3-pack for the same query set — described as AI search being "30x more selective" than traditional local search. (SOCi, "How to Rank in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overview," citing SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index) Practical read: don't expect AI-search visibility to track your Google rankings 1:1 — it needs deliberate, separate attention.
- **Reviews function as a trust gate, not a ranking gradient, for AI engines.** "AI platforms use reviews as a confidence threshold... locations recommended by ChatGPT average 4.3 stars." This reinforces Quick Win #2 (review generation) as dual-purpose work — it's not just a Google Maps tactic anymore.
- **Platform-specific mechanics differ and are worth knowing:**
  - *Google AI Overviews:* favors E-E-A-T-strong informational content targeting long-tail queries — your blog's FAQ-driven format is already the right shape for this.
  - *ChatGPT:* leans partly on Bing's index for local answers — a complete, accurate **Bing Places** listing (Quick Win #3) is a more direct lever for ChatGPT visibility than most people assume.
  - *Perplexity:* shows its sources transparently in a side panel, making it the easiest platform to audit — if you're not showing up, you can literally see what it cited instead and target getting listed/linked there.
  (soci.ai; localiq.co.uk, "Is Your Business Visible in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity?," 2026)
- **Entity consistency now matters even more.** "Google's AI Overviews and AI-powered local results in 2026 rely heavily on entity SEO — the ability to verify that a business is a distinct, real-world entity with consistent, cross-verified information." This is the same NAP-consistency work from Section 5, but frame it internally as AI-search infrastructure, not just a legacy local-SEO checkbox. (navoto.com / bippermedia.com, "AI Overviews Local Citations Checklist 2026")
- **Recommendation, not yet urgent:** some sites in 2026 are experimenting with an `llms.txt` file (a plain-text summary of site content/structure aimed at AI crawlers, analogous to robots.txt) — this is an emerging, not yet standardized, practice. Worth monitoring rather than implementing today; low cost to add later once conventions stabilize.
