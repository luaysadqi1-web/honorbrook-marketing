What Medicare covers — and where it stops

Many people assume that if they ever need help recovering at home, Medicare will simply pay for it. In reality, Medicare covers limited skilled care — the kind of short-term recovery care a doctor orders after a hospital stay or while you're getting better from an illness or injury. That coverage is meant for recovery, not for the long haul.

Where Medicare stops is custodial care — ongoing, non-medical help with everyday activities like bathing, dressing, preparing meals, and getting around. That's the type of help people are most likely to need as they age, and extended home care or assisted living can add up quickly. Original Medicare alone wasn't built to cover that gap, which is exactly where short-term care and home health care plans come in.

How short-term & home care plans help

  • They pay benefits where you need care. Plans can pay toward home health aides, assisted living, or recovery care — so you can get help at home or in a facility, whichever fits your situation.
  • They're easier on your budget. Because the benefit period is shorter, these plans are generally more affordable and easier to qualify for than traditional long-term care insurance.
  • They protect what you've built. Benefits help cover the cost of care so a health setback doesn't force you to drain your retirement savings — and so you can keep your independence.
The honest tradeoff: a short-term care plan won't cover years of care the way a full long-term care policy might — its benefit period is shorter by design. In exchange, you get coverage that's more affordable, easier to qualify for, and far better than having no protection at all. The right plan is the one whose benefit amount and length fit your budget and your goals — and that's exactly what we help you weigh, at no cost.

Who it's a fit for

Short-term and home care plans tend to fit retirees who want to stay in their own home rather than move to a facility, and who want a way to pay for help if they need it. They're also a practical choice for people who looked at traditional long-term care insurance and found it too costly, too hard to qualify for, or both. If you want meaningful protection without the price tag of a full long-term care policy, this is often the sweet spot — and we're happy to compare the options with you.

Common questions

Short-Term & Home Care FAQ

Doesn't Medicare cover home care?
Only limited skilled care, such as short-term recovery care after a hospital stay. Medicare does not pay for ongoing custodial care — the day-to-day help with bathing, dressing, meals, and similar needs that many people need at home over time.
How is this different from long-term care insurance?
Short-term care plans cover a shorter benefit period than traditional long-term care insurance. Because the coverage window is shorter, they're generally more affordable and easier to qualify for — a practical option if full long-term care insurance is too costly or hard to get.
Can I use it for care at home?
Yes — that's the point. These plans are designed to help pay for care where you want to receive it, including help from a home health aide so you can recover or get assistance in your own home rather than only in a facility.
Can I still qualify with health issues?
Often yes. Short-term care plans usually have simpler health questions than traditional long-term care insurance, so people with some health conditions can still qualify. As an independent agency, we match you to a carrier whose underwriting fits your situation.
