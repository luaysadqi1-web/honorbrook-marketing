What is a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan?

A Medicare Supplement plan — commonly called Medigap — works alongside Original Medicare (Part A and Part B). After Medicare pays its share of a covered service, your Medigap policy pays some or all of the remaining costs: deductibles, copays, and coinsurance. The result is far more predictable out-of-pocket spending and very few surprise bills.

Unlike Medicare Advantage, Medigap has no networks. You can see any doctor, specialist, or hospital in the country that accepts Original Medicare — no referrals, no in-network worries when you travel. That freedom is why Medigap appeals to people who value choice and stability.

Key fact most people don't know: Medigap plans are standardized by the federal government. A "Plan G" is the same coverage no matter which company sells it — only the price and the carrier's service differ. So shopping carriers can save you hundreds of dollars a year for the exact same benefits. Comparing those rates for you is the heart of what we do.

When should you enroll? Timing matters

Your Medigap Open Enrollment Period is a one-time, six-month window that starts the month you're 65 and enrolled in Part B. During this window you have guaranteed-issue rights — carriers cannot turn you down or charge more because of your health. This is the best time to buy.

Outside that window, carriers in most states can use medical underwriting, meaning your health history can affect approval and price. Certain situations (like losing other coverage) can also open guaranteed-issue rights. If you're unsure where you stand, we'll tell you plainly and help you act before a window closes.

Do you need a drug plan too?

Yes — Medigap does not include prescription coverage. You'll pair it with a standalone Part D drug plan. We compare Part D options against your exact medication list so your total cost — Medigap premium plus drug costs — is as low as possible.

Common questions

Medigap FAQ

What's the difference between Plan G and Plan N?
Plan G covers everything except the small annual Part B deductible — after that you typically pay nothing for covered services. Plan N has a lower premium but adds small copays for some office and ER visits and doesn't cover Part B excess charges. Plan G is the most predictable; Plan N can save money if you visit the doctor less.
Why do prices differ between carriers for the same plan?
Medigap plans are standardized by the government, so a Plan G from one carrier covers exactly the same benefits as a Plan G from another — only the price and company differ. Shopping carriers can save you hundreds per year for identical coverage, which is exactly what we do for you.
Can I be turned down for a Medigap plan?
During your Medigap Open Enrollment Period — the six months starting when you're 65 and enrolled in Part B — you have guaranteed-issue rights and can't be turned down or charged more for health reasons. Outside that window, carriers may use medical underwriting, so timing matters. We'll tell you exactly where you stand.
Does Medigap include prescription drug coverage?
No. Medigap covers gaps in Original Medicare but not prescriptions. You'd pair it with a standalone Part D drug plan, which we also compare for you to find your lowest total cost.
