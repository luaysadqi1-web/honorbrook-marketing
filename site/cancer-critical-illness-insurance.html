Cancer & critical illness: cash when you need it most

A lump-sum cash benefit paid directly to you on diagnosis — so you can focus on recovery, not bills.

What cancer & critical illness insurance does

Cancer and critical illness insurance pays you a tax-free lump sum of cash when you're diagnosed with a covered condition — typically cancer, a heart attack, or a stroke. The benefit isn't sent to a hospital or doctor; it's paid directly to you. Once it's in your hands, the money is yours to use for whatever you need most, whether that's a medical bill, the mortgage, groceries, travel to a treatment center, or simply replacing a paycheck while you recover.

Because the payout is a single, predictable sum triggered by the diagnosis itself, you don't have to itemize expenses or wait on reimbursement claims. You get the cash, and you decide how to spend it.

Why people add it

Even people with strong health coverage often add a cancer or critical illness policy, because a serious diagnosis hits the budget in ways a medical plan was never designed to cover:

  • Out-of-pocket costs add up. Deductibles, copays, coinsurance, and the plan's out-of-pocket maximum can mean real money during a long course of treatment — even with good coverage.
  • Lost income during treatment. Time away from work, reduced hours, or a caregiver taking leave can shrink household income exactly when expenses are rising.
  • Everyday bills don't pause. Rent or the mortgage, utilities, childcare, and groceries keep coming whether or not you're working.
  • Costs your health plan leaves behind. Travel and lodging for specialized care, in-home help, and other non-medical expenses usually aren't covered — the cash fills the gaps your Medicare plan leaves.
The honest tradeoff: This coverage doesn't pay your medical bills directly the way major medical does — it hands you cash on a covered diagnosis and lets you decide where it's needed most. For many people that flexibility is exactly the point: the money can go toward the bills your health plan won't touch.

How it works alongside your health plan

Cancer and critical illness insurance is supplemental coverage. It doesn't replace your medical plan and it doesn't coordinate with it — it simply pays in addition to whatever your health coverage already does. Your major medical or Medicare plan keeps handling your treatment claims, and this policy pays you a separate cash benefit on top. That's why it works as a financial cushion rather than a replacement for health insurance: the two are designed to sit side by side.

Common questions

Cancer & Critical Illness FAQ

How is the benefit paid?
As a lump sum of cash paid directly to you after a covered diagnosis. The money goes to you, not to a hospital or provider, so you decide how to use it.
Can I use the money for non-medical costs?
Yes — the cash is yours to use for anything. Many people use it for everyday bills, the mortgage or rent, travel to treatment, childcare, or simply replacing income while they recover.
Does it replace health insurance?
No. Cancer and critical illness insurance is supplemental. It pays in addition to your medical coverage and is not a substitute for major medical or Medicare coverage.
Can I qualify if I'm older?
Often yes. Many carriers offer cancer and critical illness coverage to older applicants, and qualification varies by carrier and health history. We match you to a carrier whose underwriting fits your situation.
