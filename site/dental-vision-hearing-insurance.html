Dental, vision & hearing coverage Medicare leaves out

Original Medicare covers none of these everyday needs — routine dental, vision, and hearing care are simply not part of Part A or Part B. Affordable standalone dental, vision & hearing (DVH) plans fill the gap, so cleanings, glasses, and hearing aids don't come straight out of pocket.

What Original Medicare doesn't cover

One of the biggest surprises for people new to Medicare is how much everyday care it leaves out. Original Medicare — Part A (hospital) and Part B (medical) — is built around medical and hospital services, not routine maintenance for your teeth, eyes, and ears. That means three of the most common needs in retirement aren't covered at all when they're routine.

  • Routine dental. Cleanings, exams, fillings, extractions, dentures, and crowns are not covered by Original Medicare. It only helps in narrow, medically necessary situations tied to a covered hospital procedure.
  • Routine vision. Eye exams for glasses, eyeglasses, and contacts are not covered. Medicare covers cataract surgery and some medical eye conditions, but not the routine exam-and-glasses cycle most people need.
  • Routine hearing. Hearing exams for fitting hearing aids and the hearing aids themselves are not covered — even though they're among the most common and costly needs as we age.

What a DVH plan covers

A standalone dental, vision & hearing plan is designed to fill exactly these gaps. Benefits vary by carrier and plan tier, but here's the kind of care these plans are built to help with:

Benefit areaWhat's covered
DentalCleanings, fillings, extractions, dentures, crowns
VisionEye exams, glasses, contacts allowance
HearingHearing exams, hearing aids

Most DVH plans cover preventive care — like cleanings and routine exams — quickly, then add annual allowances toward bigger-ticket items such as dentures, crowns, and hearing aids. The right plan depends on the care you actually expect to use, which is exactly what we help you sort out.

Standalone vs. built into Medicare Advantage

There are two common ways to get dental, vision, and hearing coverage. Some Medicare Advantage plans include limited DVH benefits bundled in — convenient, but often capped at modest annual amounts that don't go far on dentures or hearing aids. A standalone DVH plan is purchased separately and frequently covers more, with higher allowances and a wider choice of providers.

Standalone plans pair especially well with Original Medicare and a Medigap policy, since Medigap leaves dental, vision, and hearing out entirely. You keep the freedom to see any doctor who accepts Medicare, and add focused coverage for the everyday needs Medigap doesn't touch.

The honest tradeoff: the DVH built into a Medicare Advantage plan is "free" in the sense that it's bundled — but those benefits are usually limited, and choosing a plan for its dental perk can mean compromising on the network or drug coverage that matters more. A standalone plan costs a separate premium, but you control the level of coverage. We'll show you both so the math is clear.

Common questions

Dental, Vision & Hearing FAQ

Does Medicare cover dental, vision, and hearing?
Not for routine care. Original Medicare doesn't cover routine cleanings, fillings, dentures, eye exams for glasses, eyeglasses, routine hearing exams, or hearing aids. It only steps in for limited, medically necessary situations. A standalone DVH plan is how most people cover these everyday needs.
Can I get a DVH plan without Medicare Advantage?
Yes. Standalone dental, vision, and hearing plans are sold separately and don't require a Medicare Advantage plan. They pair well with Original Medicare and a Medigap policy, letting you keep your current doctors while still covering cleanings, glasses, and hearing aids.
Do DVH plans cover dentures and hearing aids?
Many do, though the benefit varies by plan. Stronger plans include allowances for dentures, crowns, and hearing aids, while leaner plans focus on cleanings and exams. We compare what each carrier actually pays so you choose a plan that fits the care you expect to need.
Is there a waiting period for DVH coverage?
Some services may have one. Many plans cover preventive care like cleanings and exams right away, but major services such as crowns, dentures, or hearing aids can have a waiting period. We compare waiting periods across carriers so there are no surprises before you enroll.
