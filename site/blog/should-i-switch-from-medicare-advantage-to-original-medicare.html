Should I switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare? (And can I get Medigap?)

Maybe, but check Medigap first. You can leave Medicare Advantage during Annual Enrollment (Oct 15 – Dec 7, 2026) or from Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2027. But outside a guaranteed-issue right, most states let Medigap insurers use health questions to approve you or set your price.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Plenty of people join a Medicare Advantage plan for the low premium and extras, then find the network too narrow, the referrals tiresome, or a big hospital bill looming. Going back to Original Medicare is allowed. The part that trips people up is what comes next: the Medigap (Medicare Supplement) policy that usually makes Original Medicare predictable is not guaranteed to be available to you. Here is how to think it through before you cancel anything.

The one rule to remember: leaving Advantage is easy. Getting Medigap afterward is the hard part. Per Medicare.gov, there is no guarantee an insurance company will sell you a Medigap policy if you do not meet its medical underwriting requirements, unless a guaranteed-issue right (Medicare.gov calls these "Medigap protections") applies to you. Do not drop your Advantage plan until you know whether you can be approved for Medigap.

When you can leave Medicare Advantage

  • Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7, 2026): switch to Original Medicare plus a standalone Part D plan. New coverage starts January 1, 2027. See our AEP guide.
  • Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment (Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2027): if you are already in an Advantage plan, one change, including back to Original Medicare.
  • Special Enrollment Periods: events such as moving out of the plan's service area or the plan leaving Medicare can open a window. Medicare.gov lists them on its Special Enrollment Periods page.

For every window in one place, read When Can You Change Medicare Plans?

Can you get Medigap after leaving Advantage?

It depends on which rights you have. Medicare.gov explains that when a guaranteed-issue right applies, the insurer must sell you a policy, must cover your pre-existing conditions, and cannot charge you more because of your health. When none applies, the insurer can ask health questions (medical underwriting) and may decline you or charge more.

The trial right

If you joined a Medicare Advantage plan (or PACE) when you were first eligible for Part A at 65, and you decide within the first year to return to Original Medicare, Medicare.gov says you may be able to buy any Medigap policy sold by an insurer in your state. You can apply starting 60 days before your Advantage coverage ends, up to 63 days after it ends. A second trial right can apply if you dropped a Medigap policy to join Advantage, in which case you can generally reapply for the same policy if it is still sold; if not, you can choose from certain standard plans sold in your state.

If you are past the trial period

If you have been in Advantage longer than the trial window, you are usually in underwriting territory, and the Medigap Open Enrollment Period (the six months after you are 65 and in Part B) has already passed. Approval is not automatic, and the price may reflect your health history. Some other situations create guaranteed-issue rights, such as your Advantage plan leaving Medicare or no longer serving your area, so ask before assuming you are stuck.

Original Medicare without Medigap: know the exposure

If you return to Original Medicare and cannot get a Medigap policy, you generally carry the Part A and Part B cost-sharing yourself, with no annual out-of-pocket limit like an Advantage plan has. You also need a separate Part D drug plan. That can still be the right move if your doctors do not take your Advantage plan, but go in knowing it.

In your state

A few states add protections beyond the federal rules. The ones that matter most are "birthday rules" that let current Medigap holders switch plans around their birthday. Notably, these generally help people who already have a Medigap policy, not someone coming out of Advantage with none. Here is what we can confirm from official sources, and what to check:

  • Virginia: The Code of Virginia § 38.2-3611 gives people with an individual Virginia Medigap policy a birthday window of at least 60 days to move to a policy with the same benefits without health-based denial or pricing. It applies to current policyholders. See our Virginia page.
  • Maryland: The Maryland Insurance Administration describes a yearly window covering the policyholder's birthday plus 30 days, for plans of equal or lesser value. It also applies to current Medigap policyholders. See our Maryland page.
  • North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, Alabama, Louisiana, Texas, Michigan, Indiana, West Virginia: Rules vary by state and change over time, and we have not confirmed a protection for returning Advantage members from an official source. Call us, your state Department of Insurance, or your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) before you decide. A SHIP locator is at shiphelp.org.

Check my Medigap options first

A safer order of steps

  • 1. Confirm your window. Is it AEP, MA Open Enrollment, or a Special Enrollment Period?
  • 2. Find out when you joined Advantage. This tells you whether a trial right might apply.
  • 3. Apply for Medigap before you disenroll. Medigap cannot start until Advantage coverage ends, so line up the dates to avoid a gap.
  • 4. Add Part D. Original Medicare does not include drug coverage.
  • 5. Check your doctors, drugs and costs side by side. Our Advantage vs. Medigap guide can help.

This article is general information, not insurance advice. Rules and dates vary by state and situation, and a licensed agent can confirm what applies to you.

Common questions

Leaving Medicare Advantage FAQ

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Original Medicare any time?
Not usually. The main chances are the Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7, 2026, with coverage starting Jan 1, 2027) and the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2027). A Special Enrollment Period, such as moving out of your plan's service area or your plan leaving Medicare, can also open a window.
Can I be denied a Medigap policy after leaving Medicare Advantage?
Yes, in most states, if you are outside your Medigap Open Enrollment Period and have no guaranteed-issue right. Medicare.gov notes there is no guarantee an insurance company will sell you a policy if you do not meet its medical underwriting requirements. Some states add their own protections, so rules vary by state.
What is the Medigap trial right?
If you joined a Medicare Advantage plan when you were first eligible for Medicare at 65 and decide within the first year to return to Original Medicare, you may be able to buy any Medigap policy sold in your state. Medicare.gov says to apply from 60 days before your Advantage coverage ends to 63 days after it ends.
