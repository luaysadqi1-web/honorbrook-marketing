The small business health care tax credit: do you qualify?

A federal credit can offset part of what you pay toward employee health premiums. Here's who qualifies, how it's calculated, and how to claim it.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

If you own a small business and help pay for your employees' health insurance, or you're thinking about starting, there's a federal tax credit you should know about. The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit was created under the Affordable Care Act to make coverage more affordable for smaller employers with modest payrolls. Many owners never claim it, often because they don't know it exists or assume they're too small to bother.

This guide walks through the eligibility rules in plain English, so you can tell quickly whether it's worth a closer look with your tax preparer.

The four basic requirements

According to the IRS, an employer generally must meet all four of these tests to qualify:

  • Fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent (FTE) employees. This is a calculated number, not a headcount. Two half-time employees, for example, generally count as one FTE.
  • Average annual wages below a set limit. The IRS adjusts this limit for inflation each year, so check the current figure in the instructions for Form 8941.
  • You pay at least 50% of the premium for employee-only coverage, at a uniform percentage for each enrolled employee.
  • Coverage is purchased through the SHOP Marketplace (the Small Business Health Options Program), with limited exceptions.

That last point trips people up. A small group plan purchased outside SHOP generally doesn't qualify, even if it's otherwise identical. If you're comparing paths, our guide to group plans vs. SHOP vs. ICHRA explains how each one works.

Who counts as an employee?

The FTE count has some rules that surprise owners. Generally, the following people are not counted as employees for the credit, and premiums you pay for them don't count toward it:

  • Sole proprietors, partners, and shareholders who own more than 2% of an S corporation
  • Owners of more than 5% of the business
  • Certain family members of those owners, including spouses
  • Seasonal workers who work 120 days or fewer during the year

To estimate FTEs, the IRS method is roughly this: add up the hours of service for all counted employees (capping each person at 2,080 hours), then divide by 2,080. The result, rounded down, is your FTE count. Wages for the average-wage test follow the same exclusions.

A business with only owners and family members won't qualify. Because owners and their relatives are excluded, a business needs at least one employee outside that group to claim the credit. A sole proprietor covering only themselves, for example, isn't eligible.

How much is the credit?

For eligible small businesses, the maximum credit is 50% of the premiums the employer pays. For tax-exempt employers, the maximum is 35%. Those maximums are set in the law, but most employers receive less, because the credit phases down on a sliding scale:

  • The largest credit goes to employers with 10 or fewer FTEs and average wages well below the limit.
  • The credit shrinks as your FTE count rises above 10 and as average wages rise, and it phases out completely at 25 FTEs or at the wage limit.
  • The premiums used in the calculation can also be capped at the average premium for the small group market in your state.

Because of the phase-outs, two businesses with the same number of employees can receive very different credits. The only way to know your number is to run your actual payroll figures through the calculation.

The two-year window

The credit can be claimed for two consecutive taxable years, starting with the first year you offer coverage through SHOP and claim it. That means timing matters. If your business is still growing and you expect to qualify for a larger credit next year, it may make sense to talk with your tax preparer about when to start the clock. After the two years end, you can keep the coverage, but the credit stops.

How to claim it

  1. Enroll through SHOP. SHOP plans are typically purchased through a registered agent or directly from a participating insurer. Availability varies by state and county.
  2. Keep records of hours worked, wages paid, and premiums you contributed for each employee during the year.
  3. Complete IRS Form 8941, Credit for Small Employer Health Insurance Premiums, when you file.
  4. Report it with your return. Most businesses carry the credit to Form 3800 as part of the general business credit. Tax-exempt employers claim it on Form 990-T, where it may be refundable.

One more detail: you generally can't also deduct the portion of premiums equal to the credit, so your premium deduction is reduced by the credit amount. Your tax preparer will handle that adjustment, but it's worth knowing the credit and the deduction work together.

Is it worth it for your business?

For a very small business with lower average wages, the credit can meaningfully offset the cost of starting coverage. For a business near 25 FTEs or near the wage limit, the credit may be small, and a regular group plan or an ICHRA could offer more flexibility. The credit is one factor to weigh, not the only one. Plan design, employee needs, carrier availability, and your budget all matter too.

Common questions

Small business tax credit FAQ

Can I claim the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit if I buy a plan outside SHOP?
Generally, no. To claim the credit, coverage generally must be purchased through the SHOP Marketplace. The IRS describes limited exceptions, such as when no SHOP plan is available in your area, so check with your tax preparer if that applies to you.
Do owners count toward the 25-employee limit?
Generally, no. Sole proprietors, partners, more-than-2% S corporation shareholders, more-than-5% owners, and certain family members aren't counted as employees for the credit, and premiums paid for them don't count toward it. Seasonal workers who work 120 days or fewer a year are also generally excluded from the count.
How many years can I claim the credit?
The credit is available for two consecutive taxable years, beginning with the first year you offer qualifying coverage through SHOP and claim it. After that two-year window, you can keep the coverage, but the credit ends.
