QLAC: How a Longevity Annuity Affects Your Required Minimum Distributions

A QLAC, or qualifying longevity annuity contract, is a deferred income annuity bought with IRA or plan money. While payments are deferred, its value is excluded from the balance used to figure required minimum distributions, and payments must start no later than the first day of the month after your 85th birthday. The premium limit is $200,000, adjusted for inflation.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Important: this article is educational, not personalized advice. Annuity guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability, and annuities can carry surrender charges and fees. They are not bank products or investments. Speak with a licensed professional and your tax advisor before moving money.

What the IRS requires

  • Start date. Payments must begin no later than the first day of the month after your 85th birthday.
  • Premium limit. The final regulations removed the old 25%-of-balance limit and raised the dollar limit from $125,000 to $200,000, adjusted for inflation. The adjusted figure changes, so confirm the current-year number.
  • No cash-out feature. The contract cannot make available a commutation benefit, cash surrender value, or similar feature before payments begin.
  • RMD effect. While it qualifies, the QLAC’s value is excluded from the account balance used to calculate required minimum distributions.

Source: IRS Internal Revenue Bulletin 2024-33 (final QLAC regulations).

How it connects to required withdrawals

The IRS current page says you generally must start withdrawals from an IRA or employer plan at age 73, and a required minimum distribution cannot be rolled over (IRS: required minimum distributions). The starting age depends on your birth year, so confirm yours. A QLAC lowers the balance the RMD formula uses, which can lower the required amount in the years before the QLAC pays. It does not remove RMDs on the rest of the account, and the QLAC’s payments are taxable when they arrive.

The trade-offs

  • The money is committed. Because the contract cannot offer cash surrender value, you cannot get the premium back as cash if circumstances change.
  • If you die before payments start. Whether anything is paid to a beneficiary depends on the contract. Ask about a return-of-premium feature and what it costs.
  • Inflation. A fixed payment starting at 85 buys less than the same payment would today. Ask whether the contract has an inflation adjustment and how it affects the starting amount.
  • The carrier. Payments depend on the issuing company’s claims-paying ability for decades.

Where it fits with a rollover

A QLAC is bought with money in an IRA or an employer plan, so a rollover to an IRA is often the first step if the plan does not offer one. See every rollover account at a glance and single-premium annuities for how income annuities differ.

Common questions

QLAC FAQ

How much can I put in a QLAC?
The final regulations set the limit at $200,000, adjusted for inflation, and removed the old 25%-of-balance limit. Confirm the current-year figure before buying.
Does a QLAC reduce required minimum distributions?
It can lower them while payments are deferred, because the QLAC’s value is excluded from the balance used to calculate RMDs. RMDs on the rest of the account still apply.
Can a QLAC start before 85?
Yes. The rule is that payments must begin no later than the first day of the month after your 85th birthday.
