What Is Medicare Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy), and Do I Qualify?

Medicare Extra Help, also called the Part D Low-Income Subsidy, is a federal program that lowers drug plan premiums, deductibles and copays for people with limited income and savings. For 2026, Medicare.gov lists limits of $23,940 income and $18,090 resources for an individual, and you may qualify automatically.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

If prescriptions eat up too much of your monthly budget, Extra Help may be the most valuable Medicare benefit you have not heard of. It is run by Medicare and the Social Security Administration, it does not cost anything to apply, and it works alongside a Medicare Part D drug plan. Here is what it does and who qualifies.

What Extra Help actually does

Extra Help pays part of your Part D costs. According to Medicare.gov, the cost-sharing listed for 2027 is:

  • No plan deductible and no premium (for plans at or below a benchmark amount; some plans cost more).
  • Copays of up to $5.80 for a generic drug and up to $14.40 for a brand-name drug.
  • Once total drug costs reach $2,400, no copay for each covered drug.
  • No more than $4.90 per covered drug if you have full Medicaid and QMB.

Your actual amounts depend on which Extra Help level you get. It only reduces drug costs. It does not change your Part B premium or what you pay for doctor visits. For more on how drug plans are priced, see what Part D actually costs.

Who qualifies?

Medicare.gov lists these 2026 limits, which can rise each year (Alaska and Hawaii differ):

HouseholdIncome limitResource limit
Individual$23,940$18,090
Married couple$32,460$36,100
Automatic qualification: You get Extra Help without applying if you have full-benefit Medicaid, state help with Part B premiums through a Medicare Savings Program, or Supplemental Security Income (SSI). Everyone else must apply.

How to apply

You apply through the Social Security Administration at ssa.gov. Have your income and savings information ready. If you are approved, you generally keep your current drug plan or can choose one that fits your medications. Because Extra Help only works with a Part D plan, you must be enrolled in one, either stand-alone or inside a Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage. Common myths about Extra Help

  • "I own a home, so I cannot qualify." Resource limits are about savings and similar assets, and Social Security describes them on its application page. Your decision should not rest on a guess, so apply or ask before ruling yourself out.
  • "It replaces my drug plan." It does not. Extra Help sits on top of a Part D plan, so you still need to be enrolled in one.
  • "It is only for people who are retired." Eligibility is based on Medicare enrollment, income and resources, not on whether you have stopped working.
  • "Applying could cost me something." There is no fee to apply. The worst case is that you are told you do not qualify.

What to do if you are approved

First, look at your current drug plan. Extra Help lowers your cost-sharing, but a plan that does not cover one of your medications, or that excludes your pharmacy, can still cause problems. Check that each of your prescriptions is on the plan's drug list and note any prior authorization or step therapy rules. Second, keep your records: approval letters from Social Security or your state are worth saving. Third, remember that your eligibility can be reviewed, so open and read mail from Medicare, Social Security and your state Medicaid office rather than setting it aside.

Extra Help and changing plans

Medicare.gov says that people with Extra Help or Medicaid may be able to change drug coverage once per month. Because the details can depend on your plan type and circumstances, confirm before you cancel anything. Calling 1-800-MEDICARE or our office first keeps you from ending up with a gap in drug coverage, which can trigger a penalty later.

Why a plan review still matters

Extra Help lowers what you pay, but it does not make every plan equal. Plans differ in which drugs they cover, which pharmacies are preferred, and which medications need prior approval. Medicare plans also change every year, so Annual Enrollment (October 15 to December 7) is a good time to recheck your drug list. If you also see the Annual Notice of Change from your plan, our guide on reading your ANOC can help. Missing Part D when first eligible can add a penalty, which we cover in this late enrollment penalty article.

Common questions

Medicare Extra Help FAQ

Do I have to apply for Extra Help every year?
Not usually. People who qualify automatically are generally re-checked each year and notified by mail, and others may be asked to confirm their information. Keep your address current with Social Security and watch for letters from Medicare or Social Security.
Does Extra Help cover my Part B premium or doctor visits?
No. Extra Help only lowers Part D prescription drug costs. Help with the Part B premium comes from a Medicare Savings Program, which is a separate state program. Having a Medicare Savings Program is one way to qualify for Extra Help automatically.
If I get Extra Help, can I change my drug plan outside open enrollment?
Medicare.gov says people with Extra Help or Medicaid may be able to change drug coverage once per month. Rules for your situation can differ, so confirm with 1-800-MEDICARE or call us before you switch anything.
