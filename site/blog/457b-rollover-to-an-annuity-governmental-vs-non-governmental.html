457(b) Rollover to an Annuity: Governmental vs. Non-Governmental Plans

It depends on who your employer is. A governmental 457(b), offered by a state or local government, can generally be rolled to an IRA and then used to buy an annuity. A 457(b) from a tax-exempt employer, such as a nonprofit, cannot be rolled over at all, and an attempted rollover creates an excise-tax problem.

Who offers itState and local governmentsNonprofits and other tax-exempt employers

Source: IRS comparison of 457(b) plans and the IRS issue snapshot. The IRS says the same for 457(f) plans.

How to tell which one you have

Ask your plan administrator or HR department in writing: “Is this a governmental 457(b) plan?” The summary plan description usually states the employer type. Do not guess, because the cost of guessing wrong is a tax problem, not just a delay.

The traps with a governmental 457(b)

  • You can lose the early-withdrawal advantage. The IRS says distributions from a governmental 457(b) are generally not subject to the 10% additional tax on early distributions, except for amounts that were rolled into the plan from other plans or IRAs (IRS Topic 558). Money rolled into an IRA is IRA money, and IRA withdrawals before age 59½ are subject to the 10% additional tax unless an exception applies.
  • Withholding. A taxable eligible rollover distribution paid to you from an employer plan is subject to mandatory withholding, generally 20% (IRS Topic 413). Use a direct rollover.
  • Hardship payments. Payments for an unforeseeable emergency are hardship distributions, which cannot be rolled over (IRS: Rollovers of retirement plan and IRA distributions).
  • Required minimum distributions. These cannot be rolled over either.

Where an annuity fits

An annuity is bought inside an IRA after the rollover. The IRA already defers tax, so the case for an annuity rests on its contract features, not on extra tax deferral. A younger retiree who may need to withdraw before 59½ should weigh two costs together: the loss of the 457(b) exception and any surrender charge on the annuity. Common questions

457(b) Rollover FAQ

Can I roll a nonprofit 457(b) into an IRA?
No. The IRS says distributions from a 457(b) maintained by a tax-exempt entity cannot be rolled over, and an attempted rollover is treated as an excess contribution subject to an excise tax.
Is a 457(f) plan the same as a 457(b)?
No. They are different arrangements, but the IRS says distributions from 457(f) plans cannot be rolled over either.
Will I owe the 10% additional tax after rolling a governmental 457(b) into an IRA annuity?
Not on the rollover itself. But early withdrawals from an IRA before age 59½ are generally subject to the 10% additional tax unless an exception applies, which is a difference from the 457(b).
