Small business health insurance: group plans vs. SHOP vs. ICHRA

Three ways to offer health coverage, three very different sets of rules. Here's how they compare on cost control, employee choice, and paperwork.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · September 2026

If you run a business with a handful of employees, or a few dozen, offering health coverage is one of the most effective ways to attract and keep good people. It's also one of the most confusing decisions you'll make. There are three main paths: a traditional small group plan, a plan purchased through SHOP, or an ICHRA. Each works well for a different kind of business.

Option 1: A traditional small group plan

You choose one or more plans from a carrier, contribute toward premiums, and employees enroll in the plans you picked. This is the model most people picture when they think of "work insurance."

  • Familiar and simple for employees. One plan menu and one ID card from the carrier.
  • Carrier rules apply. Carriers typically set minimum employee participation and employer contribution requirements.
  • Less cost predictability. Renewals can change year to year, and you bear that risk.
  • Limited choice for employees. They're generally limited to the carrier and networks you selected.

Option 2: SHOP (Small Business Health Options Program)

SHOP is the Marketplace channel for small employer coverage, generally for businesses with 1 to 50 employees. The plans are small group plans, but SHOP has one feature regular group coverage doesn't: it's the only way to claim the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit.

  • Potential tax credit for qualifying employers with fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent employees who pay at least half of employee-only premiums and meet average-wage limits.
  • Otherwise similar to group coverage, including participation and contribution rules.
  • Availability varies by area. Which carriers offer SHOP plans depends on your state and county. SHOP plans are typically purchased through a registered agent or directly from a participating insurer.
The tax credit is worth checking, but it isn't for everyone. It's limited to smaller employers with lower average wages, and it can generally be claimed for only two consecutive years. If you're close to the thresholds, run the numbers with your tax preparer before choosing SHOP for the credit alone.

Option 3: ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA)

With an ICHRA, you don't buy a group plan at all. You set a monthly allowance, and employees buy their own individual health insurance, then get reimbursed tax-free up to that amount. Our ICHRA vs. group cost comparison goes deeper on the math.

  • Predictable budget. You decide the allowance, so your cost doesn't hinge on a carrier renewal.
  • Employee choice. Each employee picks the plan, carrier, and network that fit them.
  • No participation minimums and no minimum business size.
  • Different allowances by employee class, such as full-time vs. part-time, within the rules.
  • Trade-off: Employees shop individual-market plans, so they need guidance, and it interacts with Marketplace subsidies in ways employees should understand.

Which one fits your business?

  • Choose a traditional group plan if your team values one simple, familiar plan, you can meet participation requirements, and you're comfortable with renewal risk.
  • Look at SHOP if you're a smaller employer with modest average wages who may qualify for the tax credit.
  • Consider an ICHRA if you want a fixed budget, have employees in different situations or locations, or can't meet group participation minimums.

Your choice isn't permanent, either. Many businesses start with one approach and switch at renewal as the team grows or budgets change. For example, a business might move from a group plan to an ICHRA to gain cost control, or add a group plan once it's large enough to meet participation requirements comfortably. Just note that there are rules against offering the same class of employees both a group plan and an ICHRA.

There isn't one right answer. The best choice depends on your budget, your team, and how much you want to manage. Our small business benefits page also covers dental, vision, and other benefits you can pair with any of the three.

What to gather before you request quotes

Whichever direction you lean, having this information ready makes quotes faster and more accurate:

  1. A census of employees with dates of birth, ZIP codes, and whether each wants coverage for a spouse or dependents.
  2. Full-time and part-time counts, and your approximate number of full-time-equivalent employees.
  3. Your budget per employee, or the percentage of premiums you're willing to contribute.
  4. Average annual wages, if you want to check tax credit eligibility.
  5. Any current plan's renewal notice, if you already offer coverage.
  6. Which employees already have coverage elsewhere, such as through a spouse, since that can affect participation calculations.

With those details, it's usually possible to see within one conversation which of the three options fits your budget and team.

Common questions

Group vs. SHOP vs. ICHRA FAQ

What is the difference between SHOP and a regular small group plan?
SHOP, the Small Business Health Options Program, is the Marketplace channel for small employer coverage. SHOP plans are small group plans, but buying through SHOP is the only way to claim the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit if your business qualifies. A small group plan bought outside SHOP works similarly but isn't eligible for that credit.
Can a small business with only a few employees offer an ICHRA?
Yes. ICHRAs have no minimum or maximum employer size, and there are no carrier participation requirements. Employees must be enrolled in individual health coverage to be reimbursed. Rules about employee classes and, for larger employers, affordability still apply, so it's worth setting one up with a knowledgeable advisor.
Do small businesses have to offer health insurance?
Employers with fewer than 50 full-time-equivalent employees generally aren't subject to the Affordable Care Act's employer mandate. Many still offer coverage to attract and keep good people. Larger employers have different obligations, so confirm your status with a benefits or tax advisor.
