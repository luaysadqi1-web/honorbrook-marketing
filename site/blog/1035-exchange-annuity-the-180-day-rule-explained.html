1035 Exchange for Annuities: What It Is and the 180-Day Rule

A 1035 exchange lets you swap one annuity contract for another without recognizing gain or loss at that moment, but it is not an IRA rollover: the money must move directly between insurers. If you move only part of a contract, IRS Rev. Proc. 2011-38 generally treats it as tax-free only if nothing is withdrawn from either contract for 180 days.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Important: this article is educational, not personalized advice. Annuity guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability, and annuities can carry surrender charges and fees. They are not bank products or investments. Speak with a licensed professional and your tax advisor before moving money.

What the rule says

  • Contract for contract. Section 1035(a)(3) provides that no gain or loss is recognized on the exchange of one annuity contract for another, when the same person or persons are the obligee under the new contract as under the old one.
  • Cash you receive is taxed. If you also receive money or other property, gain is recognized up to that amount.
  • Do not take the check. The IRS cites a ruling treating a check you receive and then endorse to a second company as a distribution, not a tax-free exchange.
  • Basis carries over. In a partial exchange, the cost basis of the original contract is allocated ratably between the original and the new contract.

Source: IRS Rev. Proc. 2011-38 (partial annuity exchanges), which covers exchanges of annuity contracts. It does not address exchanges involving life insurance or annuities held inside IRAs, so ask the carriers which rules apply to your contract.

The 180-day rule for partial exchanges

For transfers completed on or after October 24, 2011, a direct transfer of part of an annuity’s cash value to a second annuity is treated as a tax-free exchange if no amount, other than annuity payments over 10 years or more or over one or more lives, is received under either contract during the 180 days starting on the transfer date. The revenue procedure replaced an earlier 12-month period, and it removed the earlier requirement to show a qualifying life event. If a transfer fails the test, the IRS looks at the facts and circumstances.

The traps

  • A new surrender period. A new contract can start a new surrender schedule. Ask for it in writing.
  • What you give up. Ask which benefits, riders or crediting terms in the old contract will not carry over.
  • Withdrawing too soon. In a partial exchange, taking a withdrawal in the first 180 days can change the tax treatment.
  • Replacement rules. State insurance departments regulate replacing one policy or annuity with another. Ask for any disclosure forms, and compare the old and new contracts side by side.

Where it fits with rollovers

A 1035 exchange and a rollover solve different problems. A rollover moves money between retirement accounts. A 1035 exchange swaps one annuity for another. Common questions

1035 Exchange FAQ

Is a 1035 exchange taxable?
A properly completed exchange of one annuity contract for another is generally not taxed at the time of the exchange, but cash or other property you receive can be taxed.
Can I do a partial 1035 exchange?
Yes. Under Rev. Proc. 2011-38, a direct transfer of part of an annuity to a second contract is generally treated as tax-free if nothing is withdrawn from either contract for 180 days.
Is a 1035 exchange the same as a rollover?
No. A 1035 exchange swaps one annuity contract for another and must go directly between insurers. A rollover moves money between retirement plans and IRAs.
