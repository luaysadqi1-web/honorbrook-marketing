SIMPLE IRA and SEP IRA Rollover to an Annuity: The Two-Year Rule

A SEP IRA can generally move into an IRA annuity like any other IRA. A SIMPLE IRA can too, but only after two years from when you first participated in your employer’s SIMPLE plan; before that, the money can go only to another SIMPLE IRA, and breaking the rule can mean income tax plus a 25% additional tax.

How the two-year clock works

The IRS counts two years from the date you first participated in your employer’s SIMPLE IRA plan. During that period, a transfer to anything other than another SIMPLE IRA is treated as a withdrawal: you include the amount in income and pay an additional 25% tax, unless you are at least 59½ at the time of the transfer or qualify for another exception. Note the nuance: being over 59½ avoids the 25% additional tax, but the amount is still included in your income.

The traps

  • 10% becomes 25%. Withdrawals from a SIMPLE IRA before 59½ normally face a 10% additional tax, which rises to 25% within two years of first participating.
  • One rollover per 12 months. The limit applies across all your IRAs together, including SEP and SIMPLE. It does not apply to trustee-to-trustee transfers, so ask for a direct transfer (IRS: Rollovers of retirement plan and IRA distributions).
  • Do not take a check. A check payable to you can start the 60-day clock and count as your one rollover for the year.
  • Ownership paperwork. Keep the SIMPLE IRA statements that show your first participation date. Ask your plan sponsor or the custodian for it in writing.

Where an annuity fits

After a valid rollover, the money is in a traditional IRA and follows IRA rules. Buying an annuity inside it does not add tax deferral, and it can add surrender charges. For a business owner who still contributes to a SEP or SIMPLE plan, an annuity is one of several ways to hold the rolled money, not a replacement for the plan. Common questions

SIMPLE and SEP Rollover FAQ

Can I move a SIMPLE IRA into an annuity in my first two years?
Not without tax. During the first two years, the IRS says a transfer can go only to another SIMPLE IRA. Otherwise it is treated as a withdrawal and an additional 25% tax can apply.
Does the one-rollover-per-year rule apply to a direct transfer?
No. The IRS says the one-per-12-months limit does not apply to trustee-to-trustee transfers between IRAs.
If I am over 59½, does the two-year rule still matter?
Partly. The 25% additional tax does not apply if you are at least 59½, but a transfer made in violation of the two-year rule is still treated as a withdrawal included in your income.
