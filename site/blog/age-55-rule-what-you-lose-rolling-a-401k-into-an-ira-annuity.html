The Age 55 Rule: What You Can Lose Rolling a 401(k) Into an IRA Annuity

If you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, withdrawals from that employer’s 401(k) may avoid the 10% additional tax on early distributions. The IRS exceptions table shows that exception for qualified plans but not for IRAs, so rolling the money to an IRA, including an IRA annuity, can give it up. Check your timeline before you roll.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

What the IRS table shows

Where the money sitsLeave the employer at 55 or laterAge 59½ or older
Employer 401(k) or similar qualified planException appliesException applies
IRA, SEP or SIMPLE IRA, including an IRA annuityException does not applyException applies

The separation-from-service exception covers an employee who leaves their employer during or after the year they reach 55 (50 for certain public safety employees in governmental plans). Source: IRS exceptions table and IRS Topic 558.

Why this matters for a rollover

Say you leave your job at 56 and roll the 401(k) into an IRA annuity. At 57, you need money. A 401(k) withdrawal could have avoided the 10% additional tax, but an IRA withdrawal at 57 would face it unless another exception applies. The annuity can add a second cost if you take more than the contract allows without a surrender charge. The exception only affects the 10% additional tax, and withdrawals are still taxed as income.

The traps

  • The exception does not follow the money. Once the money is in an IRA, the table shows no age-55 exception for it.
  • Stacking costs. A pre-59½ withdrawal from an IRA annuity can mean income tax, the 10% additional tax and a surrender charge at the same time.
  • Your plan type. The IRS table lists the exception for qualified plans. Ask your tax professional whether your specific plan, such as a 403(b), qualifies.
  • Governmental 457(b). These plans generally are not subject to the 10% additional tax at all, which is a separate advantage that also does not follow into an IRA (IRS Topic 558: Additional tax on early distributions from retirement plans other than IRAs).

Ways people handle it

Some people roll only part of the plan to an IRA annuity and leave a bridge amount in the plan for the years before 59½. Whether your plan lets you keep money there after you leave, and how withdrawals work, is in the plan document, so ask your plan administrator. See every rollover account at a glance.

Common questions

Age 55 Rule FAQ

Does the age 55 rule apply to IRAs?
No. The IRS exceptions table shows the separation-from-service exception for qualified plans but not for IRAs.
Does the rule apply to a 403(b)?
The IRS table lists the exception for qualified plans. Ask your tax professional whether your specific 403(b) qualifies before you roll it.
Does leaving at 55 mean the withdrawals are tax-free?
No. The exception removes the 10% additional tax, but withdrawals are still taxed as income.
