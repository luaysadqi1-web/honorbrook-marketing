How do I read my Medicare Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) letter?

Your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) is a letter your Medicare Advantage or Part D plan sends each fall, due by September 30, listing what changes on January 1, 2027. Read the premium, deductible, copay, drug list and network pages first, then compare against your own needs before December 7.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

That envelope from your plan, usually titled "Annual Notice of Change," is easy to set aside as junk mail. It is one of the most useful pieces of mail you get all year. Medicare.gov describes it as the notice that lists changes in coverage, costs, and more taking effect in January, and it recommends reviewing it to decide whether your plan will still meet your needs next year. This guide walks through it in the order that matters.

Key dates for the 2027 plan year
  • By Sept 30: Plans are required to have sent the ANOC to current members (Medicare.gov). No letter yet? Contact your plan.
  • Oct 15 – Dec 7, 2026: Medicare Open Enrollment. Your plan must receive any change by Dec 7 (Medicare.gov).
  • Jan 1, 2027: New coverage and the changes in your letter take effect.

What the ANOC is, and what it is not

The ANOC is a summary of what is changing in your Medicare Advantage or Part D plan. It is not a bill, and it is not a new enrollment. It is a companion to the Evidence of Coverage (EOC), the longer document that explains everything the plan covers and what you pay. Both arrive in the fall, per Medicare.gov. Original Medicare with a Medigap policy works differently, though Medigap insurers also send their own premium notices, and your standalone drug plan sends an ANOC too.

The five sections to read first

Skip the cover letter and look for the comparison tables, usually "2026" next to "2027." Check these in order:

  • Monthly premium and deductible. Did either go up or down? Also check whether the Part B premium give-back, if your plan has one, is still offered.
  • Maximum out-of-pocket (MOOP). This is your yearly ceiling on covered in-network medical costs. A lower premium with a higher ceiling can cost more in a bad year.
  • Doctor, specialist, and hospital copays. Look at the services you actually use, such as primary care visits, physical therapy, imaging, or an ER visit.
  • Prescription drug coverage. Find each of your medications on the plan's drug list (formulary). Check its tier, whether it now needs prior authorization or step therapy, and whether any pharmacy is no longer preferred. See our Part D page.
  • Provider network. Plans can change which doctors and hospitals are in network. Confirm your doctors and pharmacy are still included before January.

Extra benefits are easy to overlook

Dental, vision, hearing, over-the-counter allowances, and fitness benefits can change from year to year, and so can the dollar limits. If you chose a Medicare Advantage plan partly for one of these, find that line in the letter and confirm it is unchanged. Our AEP 2026 guide explains why plans adjust benefits each year.

What if you do nothing?

According to CMS, if you keep your current Medicare health and drug coverage you do not need to act, and it continues automatically. The risk is that "automatic" also means any changes in the ANOC apply to you on January 1. If the letter shows a drug moving to a higher tier or a doctor leaving the network, the Open Enrollment window is when you can respond. If you decide to switch, the enrollment window guide covers what you can change and when. If you are already in Advantage, there is also a Jan 1 – Mar 31 Open Enrollment Period for one more change.

Questions to ask before December 7

  • Are all my medications still covered, and at what cost?
  • Are my doctors, hospital, and pharmacy still in network?
  • What is the new out-of-pocket maximum?
  • Do any benefits I rely on look different from last year?
  • Have I compared the plan against others available in my county on Medicare Plan Finder?

Common questions

ANOC FAQ

When is my Medicare ANOC supposed to arrive?
Medicare Advantage and Part D plans are required to send the ANOC to current members by September 30, according to Medicare.gov and CMS. If you have not received yours, contact your plan and ask for a copy, or look for it on the plan's website.
What if I do nothing after reading my ANOC?
According to CMS, if you choose to keep your current Medicare health and drug coverage you do not need to do anything, and your coverage continues automatically. That is why it matters to read the letter: your plan may have changed even if you didn't.
What is the difference between the ANOC and the Evidence of Coverage?
The ANOC summarizes what is changing for next year. The Evidence of Coverage (EOC) is the longer document with full details on what the plan covers and what you pay. Medicare.gov says both are sent in the fall.
