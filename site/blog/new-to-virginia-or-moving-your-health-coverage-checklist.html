New to Virginia (or moving)? Your health coverage checklist

A move can change your plan options, your network, and your deadlines. Here's how to keep your coverage on track.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Moving is already a long to-do list: utilities, address changes, school records. Health insurance is the item people most often leave for later — and it's the one with a deadline attached. Because most health plans are tied to where you live, a move can change which plans you can buy, which doctors are in network, and how long you have to act. Here's a plain-English checklist, whether you're relocating to Virginia or just across state lines.

Why a move changes your health coverage

Individual and family plans are sold by service area, usually a state and often a county or ZIP code. If you move out of your plan's service area, that plan may no longer be a good fit — or may not be available to you at all. Moving also matters for Medicaid, which is run state by state and does not simply transfer with you. Employer plans depend on where the plan operates and where your network is.

Watch the clock: a qualifying move can open a Special Enrollment Period — commonly 60 days from the date of your move — but waiting until the next Open Enrollment may leave you without coverage in the meantime. Start early, ideally before moving day.

Your move-day coverage checklist

  1. Write down your move date and new address. The date of the move is typically what starts your enrollment window, and the new ZIP code determines which plans you can see.
  2. Check your current plan's service area. Call the insurer or read your plan documents to see whether you'll still be covered at the new address.
  3. Confirm your proof of prior coverage. Many move-related enrollment windows require that you had qualifying coverage shortly before the move. Keep a copy of your current insurance card or a coverage letter.
  4. Update your address with your plan and the Marketplace. An outdated address can affect subsidies, notices, and eligibility.
  5. Compare plans available at the new address. Prices, carriers, and networks can differ meaningfully from one state or county to the next.
  6. Check your doctors and prescriptions. Confirm that new providers near you are in network and that your medications are on the plan's drug list.
  7. Re-estimate your income. A new job, a new cost of living, or a change in household size can change the premium tax credit you're eligible for.
  8. Choose a start date that avoids gaps. Marketplace move-related enrollments typically start the first of the month after you pick a plan, so overlapping with your old coverage is worth planning for.

What to do with each type of coverage

  • Marketplace (ACA) plan: Report the move and look at plans in your new location. You may be able to keep your subsidy eligibility but will likely need a plan sold in your new area. See our ACA health insurance page.
  • Employer plan: Ask HR whether your plan has a network in the new area, or whether the move is tied to a new employer with new benefits.
  • COBRA: You may keep the plan, but the network may not follow you. Compare it against local options before committing. Our guide to losing job coverage explains the trade-offs.
  • Medicaid: Coverage does not move with you. Apply in your new state as soon as you establish residency. See how Virginia decides between ACA and Medicaid.

Moving to Virginia: a few things to know

Virginia is a short drive from Maryland and D.C., so a lot of Northern Virginia households cross state lines for work, school, or doctors. That makes the network check especially important: a plan that works well in one jurisdiction may have fewer in-network options in another. If you work in one state and live in another, the address where you live generally determines your Marketplace eligibility. Marketplace enrollment steps differ by state, so confirm where to enroll for your new address — a local agent can point you to the right place.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming your old plan will cover you at the new address.
  • Waiting for Open Enrollment when you may already have a Special Enrollment window.
  • Forgetting to update the address on file, which can delay notices or affect subsidies.
  • Skipping the doctor and prescription check until after you enroll.

Common questions

Moving & Health Coverage FAQ

Does moving to a new state let me change my health plan?
In most cases, yes. A permanent move to a new ZIP code or county that changes which plans are available to you generally opens a Special Enrollment Period, usually 60 days from the date of the move. Moving within the same plan service area typically does not. Rules can vary, so confirm your situation before relying on the window.
Do I need to have had insurance before my move to qualify?
For a Marketplace move-related Special Enrollment Period, you generally need to have had qualifying coverage for at least one day in the 60 days before you moved, with some exceptions such as moving from outside the U.S. If you were uninsured, ask a licensed agent whether you still have an option.
Will my old plan work in my new state?
Often only partially. Many plans use local provider networks and are sold within specific service areas, so coverage outside that area may be limited to emergencies. Check your plan's service area and network before you assume it follows you.
