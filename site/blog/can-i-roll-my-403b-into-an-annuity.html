Can I Roll My 403(b) Into an Annuity? Rules, Taxes and Traps

Usually yes. A 403(b) distribution can generally be rolled tax-free to a traditional IRA, and an annuity can then be bought inside that IRA, but only if your plan lets you take the money out and the payment is an eligible rollover distribution. Hardship payments, required minimum distributions and lifetime installment payments cannot be rolled.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Important: this article is educational, not personalized advice. Annuity guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability, and annuities can carry surrender charges and fees. They are not bank products or investments. Speak with a licensed professional and your tax advisor before moving money.

When can you take money out of a 403(b)?

Under IRS Publication 571, payments from a 403(b) generally cannot begin until you reach age 59½, leave your employer, die or become disabled, or, for your own contributions, face a financial hardship, plus a few special cases added in recent years (Pub. 571). So a teacher or nurse who is still working usually cannot roll over an active 403(b) unless the plan allows it. The plan document decides, so ask your plan administrator.

What can and cannot be rolled over

  • Can be rolled: all or part of an eligible distribution to a traditional IRA or another non-Roth eligible plan, tax-free.
  • Roth IRA: a distribution can be converted to a Roth IRA by direct rollover, but the converted amount is generally included in that year’s taxable income.
  • Cannot be rolled tax-free: required minimum distributions, payments that are a series of substantially equal payments over your life, the joint lives of you and a beneficiary, or 10 years or more, hardship distributions, and corrective distributions of excess contributions.

The traps

  • Withholding. A taxable eligible rollover distribution paid to you from an employer plan is subject to mandatory withholding, generally 20%, even if you intend to roll it over (IRS Topic 413). Ask for a direct rollover instead.
  • The age 55 exception. The IRS lists an exception to the 10% additional tax for leaving your employer in or after the year you turn 55 for qualified plans but not for IRAs (IRS exceptions table). If you are between 55 and 59½ and may need cash, ask your tax professional whether your 403(b) qualifies before you roll it to an IRA.
  • Contract exchanges are different. A 403(b) can be held in annuity contracts, and the IRS has separate rules for exchanging one 403(b) contract for another inside the plan (IRS 403(b) FAQs). If your 403(b) is already an annuity, ask the current issuer about surrender charges before you move it.
  • Loans and after-tax money. A loan treated as a distribution cannot be rolled over. If any of your balance is after-tax money, ask the plan how it can be rolled.

What the annuity changes

Once the money is in a traditional IRA, it follows IRA rules, including required minimum distributions, whether it sits in an annuity or not. The 403(b) did not need an annuity to defer tax, so the question is whether you want the contract features and can accept the surrender charges. Questions to ask your 403(b) plan

  • Am I eligible to take a distribution now, and under which event?
  • Can the plan send a direct rollover to an IRA, and what paperwork does the receiving company need?
  • Are any of my contracts annuities with surrender charges, and what is the schedule?
  • Do I have a loan outstanding, or any after-tax money?
  • What happens to the money if I leave it in the plan instead?

Common questions

403(b) Rollover FAQ

Can I roll a 403(b) into an annuity while I still work?
Only if your plan permits a distribution. The IRS lists age 59½, leaving your employer, death, disability and hardship among the events that can allow payments. Check your plan document.
Is a 403(b) rollover to an annuity taxable?
A direct rollover to a traditional IRA is generally not taxable when done correctly. Converting the money to a Roth IRA is generally taxable as income in the year of the distribution.
My 403(b) is already an annuity. Can I still move it?
Often yes, but ask the current issuer about surrender charges and about how contract exchanges within a 403(b) differ from a rollover to an IRA.
