Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap: how to choose the right one in 2026

It's the biggest decision you'll make in Medicare — and there's no single "better" answer. Here's the honest, plain-English breakdown of how the two paths really differ, and how to tell which one fits your health, budget, and doctors.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · June 2026

When you become eligible for Medicare, you reach a fork in the road. Down one path is Medicare Advantage (Part C); down the other is Original Medicare paired with a Medicare Supplement — better known as Medigap. Both are legitimate, popular choices. The trouble is that the marketing around them is loud, and the real tradeoffs are quiet. Let's fix that.

The 30-second version

  • Medicare Advantage bundles your hospital, medical, and usually drug coverage into one plan from a private insurer — often with a $0 monthly premium and extras like dental and vision. In exchange, you use the plan's provider network and pay copays as you go, up to a yearly out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Medigap keeps you on Original Medicare and adds a policy that pays most of the costs Medicare leaves behind. You pay a higher monthly premium, but you can see any doctor in the country who accepts Medicare, with very predictable out-of-pocket costs.
The honest truth: neither plan is "better." One trades a low monthly cost for networks and copays; the other trades a higher premium for freedom and predictability. The right choice is simply the one whose tradeoffs match your life.

Five questions that usually settle it

Instead of arguing which plan "wins," ask yourself these:

  1. How attached are you to your doctors? If you have specialists you love or you split time between states, Medigap's go-anywhere freedom is hard to beat. If your doctors are local and in-network, Advantage may serve you well.
  2. How do you feel about surprise bills? Medigap is built for people who would rather pay a steady premium than face a copay every time they need care. Advantage costs less month to month but asks you to pay as you go.
  3. Do you travel often? Original Medicare + Medigap travels with you nationwide. Advantage networks are usually regional.
  4. What's your monthly budget? If a higher fixed premium is tough, Advantage's low premium can be a real advantage — as long as you understand the copays.
  5. Are you healthy now? This is the sleeper question — see below.

The timing trap most people miss

When you first enroll at 65, you have a one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period where you can buy any Medigap plan with guaranteed-issue rights — no health questions, no denials. Choose Advantage first and later try to switch to Medigap, and in most states you can face medical underwriting, where your health history affects whether you're approved and what you pay.

Why this matters: it's often easier to move from Medigap to Advantage than the other way around. If you're healthy at 65 and value the option to keep Medigap long-term, that's worth weighing now — not after a diagnosis changes your choices.

Don't forget drug coverage

If you go the Medigap route, you'll add a standalone Part D drug plan. The cheapest Part D plan isn't the one with the lowest premium — it's the one that covers your specific medications for the lowest total annual cost. Common questions

Advantage vs. Medigap FAQ

Is Medicare Advantage or Medigap cheaper?
Advantage usually has a lower monthly premium (often $0 plus your Part B premium); Medigap has a higher premium but much lower, more predictable costs when you use care. The cheaper option overall depends on how often you need care — we compare your total expected cost, not just the premium.
Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap later?
Sometimes, but it's not guaranteed. Outside your initial Medigap window, switching can require medical underwriting in most states, so your health history can affect approval and price. That's why the first choice matters — and why it helps to talk it through before you enroll.
Does Medigap include prescription drug coverage?
No — you pair Medigap with a standalone Part D plan. Most Advantage plans include drug coverage. We compare the total cost either way.
