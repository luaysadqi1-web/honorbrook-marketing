How Much Does Final Expense Insurance Cost at 65, 70 or 75? What Sets the Price

There is no single price. Here are the factors that set it, so you can compare quotes sensibly.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Almost every family asks the same question first: how much will it cost? With final expense insurance, the honest answer is that it depends, and anyone quoting a flat price without asking questions is guessing. This guide explains what moves the price, so you can understand quotes when you see them. It does not include specific prices because they vary by carrier, state, and individual.

What final expense insurance is

Final expense insurance is a small whole life policy designed to pay for funeral costs, medical bills, and other end-of-life expenses. It typically has a smaller death benefit than traditional life insurance, simplified underwriting, and premiums that stay level for life. Learn more on our final expense page.

The factors that set your premium

  1. Age at purchase. The older you are when you buy, the higher the premium. A person at 75 will usually pay more than one at 65 for the same coverage.
  2. Coverage amount. A larger death benefit costs more. Many families choose an amount near expected funeral and final costs.
  3. Sex. Statistically, women live longer, so premiums are often lower for women.
  4. Tobacco use. Tobacco users pay more.
  5. Health. Answers to health questions affect which product and price you qualify for.
  6. Type of underwriting. This is the biggest factor and is explained below.
  7. Carrier. Companies price differently, so comparing several matters.
  8. State. Rules and rates vary by state.

Three levels of underwriting

  • Simplified issue. You answer a handful of health questions with no exam. If you qualify, the benefit is typically paid in full from day one, and premiums are lower.
  • Graded benefit. For people with some health issues, the death benefit may be reduced for the first couple of years. See what a graded death benefit is.
  • Guaranteed issue. No health questions, so anyone in the age range can qualify, but premiums are higher and early benefits are limited.
Healthy at 65? You will generally get better value from a simplified-issue or fully underwritten policy than from guaranteed-issue, so do not default to the no-questions option just because it sounds easy.

Why age matters so much

Each year you wait, the premium for the same policy rises, and your health can change in ways that limit your choices. People often start with a plan to wait and find that a diagnosis later narrows the options. That does not mean you should rush into the wrong policy, only that waiting has a cost.

Level premiums and the "lock-in"

Most whole life final expense policies have premiums that stay the same for life, and a death benefit that does not decrease. Some also build a small cash value. Confirm in the policy that premiums are level and guaranteed. Be cautious with products whose premiums can increase over time.

How to compare quotes fairly

  • Ask for the same coverage amount from every carrier.
  • Make sure the underwriting class (simplified vs. graded vs. guaranteed) is the same.
  • Check whether the death benefit is immediate or graded.
  • Ask whether premiums are guaranteed level for life.
  • Look at the insurer's financial strength ratings.
  • Check what happens if you stop paying: some policies offer reduced paid-up benefits.

How much coverage do you need?

Think about funeral and burial costs, outstanding medical bills, small debts, and whether you want to leave something extra for family. Our guide to funeral costs can help you estimate. Avoid buying more than you can comfortably afford to keep paying.

Compare with other options

Some people use a prepaid funeral plan or burial insurance instead. See final expense vs. burial insurance vs. prepaid funeral plans for the differences.

Example of how to read a quote

When you receive a quote, check that it lists the age, sex, tobacco status, coverage amount, underwriting class, and whether the benefit is immediate or graded. Compare these line by line with other quotes. Two quotes can look very different simply because one assumes a graded class and another assumes immediate benefits. A lower-looking premium may be buying you less. If a quote does not list these details, ask for them in writing.

Affordability matters more than the lowest price

The best policy is one you can keep paying. A policy that lapses after a few years pays nothing, and the premiums are lost. Choose a coverage amount that fits comfortably in your monthly budget, and set up automatic payments, ideally from an account that will not be overdrawn, so you do not miss a payment.

Common questions

Final Expense Cost FAQ

Why is final expense insurance more expensive at older ages?
Premiums rise with age because the likelihood of a claim increases. Buying earlier locks in a lower rate for the life of the policy in most cases.
Does my health affect the price of final expense insurance?
Yes. Policies that ask health questions generally cost less than guaranteed-issue policies with no questions, which carry higher prices or reduced early benefits.
Do final expense premiums go up over time?
Many whole life final expense policies have level premiums that do not increase, but you should confirm that in the policy and ask whether any rate guarantees apply.
