Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment (Jan 1 – Mar 31): Can I Switch Plans?

If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan and January brought surprises, you get one more chance to fix it.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Many people find out in January that something changed: a doctor left the network, a drug moved to a higher tier, or the copays are not what they expected. If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you may not be stuck for the year. The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, often called MA-OEP, gives you one more chance to make a change. Here is exactly how it works.

The short answer

If you are already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can make one change between January 1 and March 31. That is true in every state. You can switch to a different Advantage plan, or drop your plan and return to Original Medicare.

Who qualifies

  • People enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan (including HMO, PPO, and most special needs plans, subject to their own rules).
  • It applies to people who joined the plan during AEP or earlier.

Who does not

  • People on Original Medicare who want to move into Medicare Advantage. This window does not let you do that.
  • People who want to change a stand-alone Part D plan, unless they are in an Advantage plan and dropping back to Original Medicare.
  • Anyone who already used a change during the window.
One change only. Make sure the new plan covers your doctors and drugs before you switch, because you generally cannot undo the change during the same window.

What you can do during MA-OEP

  1. Switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan. If you want drug coverage, choose a plan that includes it.
  2. Drop your Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare. You can add a stand-alone Part D plan at the same time if you want drug coverage.

When changes take effect

Changes made during the window generally take effect the first day of the month after the plan receives your request. Keep your old coverage until the new coverage is confirmed.

The Medigap catch if you return to Original Medicare

If you leave Advantage for Original Medicare, you may want a Medigap plan. Outside certain protected situations, insurers in many states can ask health questions and may decline or charge more. Read when you can change your Medigap plan before you cancel anything.

How to decide if it is worth switching

  • Is your doctor still in network? Check the plan's directory by name.
  • Is each of your drugs covered, and at what tier?
  • Did the copays or the out-of-pocket maximum change?
  • Is the problem fixable by switching to a different Advantage plan, or do you really want Original Medicare?
  • Do you travel or spend part of the year in another state?

If the answer to most of these is "it is fine," do nothing. Switching only helps if the new plan fits better.

Compare with other windows

  • Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7): you can change almost any plan type for the following January 1.
  • Special Enrollment Periods: triggered by events like moving, losing coverage, or your plan ending.
  • Initial Enrollment Period: around your 65th birthday.

A short checklist for January and February

  1. Review your plan documents and recent bills.
  2. List doctors, hospitals, and medications.
  3. Check them against two or three alternatives.
  4. Confirm the effective date and how you will get your new card.
  5. Do not cancel anything until the new plan confirms your enrollment.

What to do if your situation is different

Some people hope to use this window to move from Original Medicare into an Advantage plan. That is not what it is for, so if that is your goal, you generally need to wait for the Annual Enrollment Period in the fall or check whether a Special Enrollment Period applies. If you are not sure which window you are in, ask before you change anything, because a wrong move can leave you with a gap or a plan you did not want.

Documents worth having handy

Keep your current plan card, your list of doctors and medications, and any recent letters from the plan. Those make it quick to compare alternatives and to confirm what will change and what will not.

Watch out for pressure tactics

During enrollment windows, calls and mailings can be aggressive. You do not have to act on a cold call, and you should never give personal details to someone you did not contact. If you want to compare plans, call a licensed agent you trust or Medicare directly, and take your time. A real change is worth a short, careful comparison, not a rushed decision.

Common questions

Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment FAQ

When is the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period?
It runs from January 1 through March 31 each year for people already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan.
Can I switch from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage during this period?
No. The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period is for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. It generally does not let you move from Original Medicare into Advantage.
How many changes can I make?
One. You can switch to another Medicare Advantage plan or drop your plan and return to Original Medicare, and you can add a stand-alone Part D plan if you return to Original Medicare.
