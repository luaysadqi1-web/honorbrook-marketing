When Can I Change My Medicare Advantage or Medigap Plan in Texas?

The Medicare windows are federal, but Medigap switching rules vary by state. Here is what applies in Texas.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

If you live in Texas and you have been searching for how and when to change your Medicare Advantage or Medigap plan, you have probably found confusing answers, because the rules depend on which type of plan you have. Here is a clear Texas-specific guide that separates the two, with the federal windows that apply everywhere and what to check for Texas in particular.

The quick answer for Texas

  • Medicare Advantage: you can generally change during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 to December 7), during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31) if you are already in an Advantage plan, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you qualify. These windows are set by Medicare, so they are the same in Texas as in every state.
  • Medigap: there is no annual deadline, but whether you can switch without health questions depends on protections that include some state rules. See below for what we found for Texas.

Medicare Advantage changes in Texas

  1. Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7). Change plans for the following January 1. See our Annual Enrollment guide.
  2. Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment (Jan 1 – Mar 31). If you are already in an Advantage plan, you get one change. Read the full guide.
  3. Special Enrollment Periods. Triggered by moving to a new area, losing other coverage, your plan ending, or qualifying for Extra Help or Medicaid, among others. See when you can switch plans.

Medigap changes in Texas

In the recent 2026 sources we reviewed, Texas was not listed as having a Medigap birthday rule. That generally means that outside your Medigap Open Enrollment Period and federal guaranteed-issue situations, an insurer may ask health questions when you apply to change plans. Rules can change, so confirm with the Texas Department of Insurance, and do not cancel your current plan until a new one is approved.

Regardless of state, you generally have protection in your six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period and in certain federal guaranteed-issue situations. Our national guide, when can I change my Medigap plan, explains each one.

Apply first, cancel second. Whatever the rule in Texas, wait for written approval of your new plan before ending the old one so you never have a gap.

What to think about in Texas

Texas is huge, and plan availability varies a great deal by county, from big metros like Houston and Dallas–Fort Worth to rural West Texas. Distances to specialists can be long, so network details matter. If you split your time between two cities or live near a county line, compare options for the exact address that your plan will use. We serve people in and around Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, El Paso and the Rio Grande Valley, and we handle everything by phone.

Free help in Texas

Texas's State Health Insurance Assistance Program is HICAP (Health Information, Counseling and Advocacy Program), which offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling. For questions about Medigap rules and consumer protections, you can also contact the Texas Department of Insurance. Program names and contact details can change, so confirm through Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE.

Common situations people ask about

  • "My doctor left my plan's network." You may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period in some cases, and you can compare plans at the next window. See what to do when a plan drops your doctor.
  • "My premium or drug costs went up." Review your Annual Notice of Change and compare during the next open window.
  • "I moved within Texas." A move to a different county can change which plans you can choose, and may open a Special Enrollment Period.
  • "I want to move from Advantage back to Original Medicare." Check your Medigap options before you cancel. Read leaving Medicare Advantage.
  • "I have both Medicare and Medicaid." You may have more frequent change opportunities. See Medicaid and dual-eligible options.
  • "I live near a county line." Plan service areas follow counties, so confirm the plan is offered where you actually live.

A checklist before you change anything in Texas

  1. Identify which window you are in (AEP, MA-OEP, SEP, or Medigap rules).
  2. List your doctors, hospitals, pharmacies, and medications.
  3. Check each against the plan you are considering at your Texas address.
  4. If it is Medigap, find out whether underwriting applies and apply before you cancel.
  5. Confirm the effective date in writing.

Changing Medicare Plans in Texas FAQ

When can I change my Medicare Advantage plan in Texas?
You can generally change during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 to December 7), during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31) if you are already in an Advantage plan, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you qualify. These windows are the same in Texas as in every state.
Can I change my Medigap plan any time in Texas?
You can generally apply any time of year, but whether you can switch without health questions depends on protections such as your Medigap Open Enrollment Period, federal guaranteed-issue rights, and any state rule. Confirm current Texas rules with the Texas Department of Insurance.
Where can I get free Medicare help in Texas?
Texas's State Health Insurance Assistance Program, HICAP (Health Information, Counseling and Advocacy Program), offers free, unbiased counseling, and an independent agent like Honorbrook can compare plans available at your address at no cost to you.
