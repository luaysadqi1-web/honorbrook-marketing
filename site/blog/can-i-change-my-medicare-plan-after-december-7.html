Can I Change My Medicare Plan After December 7? Your Options

Short answer: sometimes. The Annual Enrollment Period ends December 7, but other doors may still be open depending on your situation.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

If you are reading this after the Annual Enrollment Period (AEP) closed on December 7, you are probably worried that you are stuck with a plan that is wrong for 2027. Sometimes you are, and sometimes you are not. The honest answer depends on what type of Medicare coverage you have and what changed in your life. Here are the windows that can still open after December 7.

First, a quick refresher on AEP

The Annual Enrollment Period runs every year from October 15 to December 7. During AEP anyone with Medicare can join, drop, or switch a Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D drug plan, and changes take effect on January 1. You can read more on our Annual Enrollment page. After AEP closes, most people are locked into their choices for the rest of the year unless another window applies.

Window 1: Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment (Jan 1 – Mar 31)

If you are already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you get one more chance. Between January 1 and March 31 you can:

  • Switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan, or
  • Drop your Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare (and add a stand-alone Part D plan if you want drug coverage).

You only get one change during this window, and it does not let you move from Original Medicare into Advantage. If you suspect your plan changed in ways you do not like, this is the window to know about.

Know the limit: this window is for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. If you have Original Medicare plus a drug plan, it generally does not apply to you.

Window 2: Special Enrollment Periods (SEPs)

A Special Enrollment Period lets you change coverage outside the normal calendar when something qualifying happens. Common triggers include:

  • You move to a new ZIP code or county that is outside your plan's service area, or you move into or out of a facility.
  • You lose other coverage, such as employer coverage that was working with Medicare.
  • Your plan leaves Medicare or stops serving your area.
  • You qualify for Extra Help or you have both Medicare and Medicaid, which can create more frequent change opportunities.
  • Your plan has a high Medicare quality rating, which in some cases lets you move into it once during the year.

SEP rules are specific, and the length of the window varies. If you think one applies, do not guess. Call before you change anything so your current coverage is not cancelled by mistake.

Window 3: Your Medigap rights

Medicare Supplement (Medigap) works differently. There is no annual deadline to buy or change a Medigap plan, but outside your six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period (which starts when you are 65 and enrolled in Part B) and a few guaranteed-issue situations, insurers in most states can ask health questions and may decline or charge more based on your answers. A few states have more generous rules. Because of that, never cancel a Medigap plan until a replacement has been approved.

What if none of these windows apply?

You may need to wait until next October 15. In the meantime, there are still practical steps that can protect you:

  1. Check your plan documents. Compare the Annual Notice of Change to what your doctors and drugs actually require.
  2. Ask about exceptions. If a drug you need is not covered, you can ask your plan for a formulary exception. If a doctor is out of network, ask about continuity-of-care options.
  3. Review costs again. Know your deductible, copays, and annual out-of-pocket maximum so you can budget before a surprise.
  4. Set a reminder. Put October 15 on your calendar so you can start comparing early.

A note on timing

Plan changes made during the Annual Enrollment Period generally take effect January 1. Changes made in the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period generally take effect the first of the following month. If you are changing because of a Special Enrollment Period, the start date depends on when you enroll. Always ask for the exact effective date in writing, and keep your current coverage until the new coverage is confirmed.

How to decide whether to change

Do not change plans just because you can. Switching only helps if the new plan fits your doctors, prescriptions, travel habits, and budget. A quick comparison takes a few minutes and avoids a second mistake. Before you switch, confirm your preferred doctors are in the new plan's network, your medications are on its drug list at a cost you can afford, and the plan serves your ZIP code year-round.

Common questions

Changing Medicare After Dec 7 FAQ

Can I change Medicare plans in January?
If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can generally make one change between January 1 and March 31 during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period. If you have Original Medicare with a stand-alone drug plan, this window usually does not let you switch drug plans.
What if I miss Medicare open enrollment completely?
Your current coverage generally continues into the new year, but it may change in price, network, or drug list. You may still qualify for a Special Enrollment Period if you move, lose coverage, or meet another qualifying condition.
Can I switch Medigap plans after December 7?
Medigap plans do not have a fixed annual window the way Advantage and drug plans do, but outside certain guaranteed-issue situations the insurer may use medical underwriting in most states, so it is best to check before dropping any coverage.
