When can you actually switch Medicare plans?

"Open enrollment" isn't one date on the calendar — Medicare actually has five different windows, each with its own rules for what you can change. Here's what each one does, in plain English, so you know which one applies to you.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · August 2026

Ask five people when Medicare's "open enrollment" is and you'll likely get five different answers — and all five might be right, because Medicare doesn't have just one enrollment window. It has five, each covering a different situation: turning 65, changing plans for next year, fixing a Medicare Advantage plan you're unhappy with, adding a Medigap policy, or reacting to a life change. Mixing them up is one of the most common (and costly) Medicare mistakes. Here's each one, laid out plainly.

1. Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

This is your first window into Medicare — a seven-month period centered on your 65th birthday (three months before, your birthday month, and three months after). This is when most people enroll in Part A and Part B for the first time, and choose between Medicare Advantage or Original Medicare plus a Medigap policy.

2. Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)

October 15 – December 7 every year. This is the big one most people mean when they say "open enrollment." During AEP, you can switch from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan (or back), change from one Advantage plan to another, or switch Part D drug plans. Changes take effect January 1.

3. Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA OEP) — the one people forget

January 1 – March 31 every year. If you're already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you get one additional chance to switch — either to a different Advantage plan or back to Original Medicare (with the option to add a standalone Part D plan). This window is easy to miss because it isn't advertised nearly as loudly as AEP, but it's a genuine second chance if you picked the wrong plan in the fall.

Why this window matters: a plan that looked right on paper in November can turn out to have the wrong network or a drug that isn't covered once you're actually using it in January. MA OEP exists specifically so a rushed AEP decision doesn't lock you in for a full year. One important limit: you can only use MA OEP if you're already in a Medicare Advantage plan — it doesn't let you switch into Advantage from Original Medicare, and it doesn't let you change Medigap policies.

4. Medigap Open Enrollment Period

A one-time, six-month window that starts the month you're 65 and enrolled in Part B. During this window, you have guaranteed-issue rights — carriers can't turn you down or charge you more because of your health. Unlike the other windows, Medigap has no annual deadline; you can apply any time of year. But outside your initial window, most states allow carriers to use medical underwriting, so timing your first Medigap purchase matters more than people expect.

5. Special Enrollment Periods (SEP)

Life doesn't wait for October. SEPs open outside the usual calendar when specific events happen — moving out of your plan's service area, losing employer coverage, qualifying for Extra Help or Medicaid, or your plan losing its contract with Medicare, among others. A SEP can open a short window (often 2-3 months) to make a plan change tied to that specific event.

Ask about my enrollment window

A quick way to figure out where you stand

  • Turning 65 soon? You're in your Initial Enrollment Period — the biggest decision window you'll get.
  • Already have a plan and it's fall? You're in AEP — this is when to review, not just renew.
  • It's January through March and you have Medicare Advantage? You still get one more shot to fix a plan that isn't working.
  • Thinking about Medigap for the first time? Check how long it's been since you turned 65 and enrolled in Part B — your guaranteed-issue window may already be ticking.
  • Just moved, retired, or lost coverage? Ask us whether that opened a Special Enrollment Period — it often does.

Common questions

Enrollment Windows FAQ

What happens if I miss my Medicare enrollment window?
It depends which one. Miss your Initial Enrollment Period and you may face a late-enrollment penalty added to your Part B or Part D premium for as long as you have coverage. Miss AEP and you're generally locked into your current plan until next year, unless a Special Enrollment Period applies to you.
Can I switch Medicare Advantage plans more than once a year?
Usually not. Outside Special Enrollment Periods, you generally get two chances: AEP (Oct 15 – Dec 7) and MA Open Enrollment (Jan 1 – Mar 31), which allows one additional switch if you're already in an Advantage plan.
Does Medigap have an annual enrollment deadline?
No — you can apply for Medigap any time of year. What matters most is your one-time, six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, which starts the month you're 65 and enrolled in Part B, when you have guaranteed-issue rights.
