Can I Keep My ACA Subsidy If My Income Changes During the Year?

Your subsidy is based on an estimate. If reality turns out different, you settle up on your tax return, so updating early helps.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

One of the biggest worries for people on Marketplace plans with subsidies is what happens if their income changes. Freelancers, commission earners, seasonal workers, and anyone who changes jobs mid-year all face it. The short version: your monthly subsidy is based on an estimate, and the true-up happens when you file taxes. Understanding that process helps you avoid unpleasant surprises.

Rules change. Subsidy amounts, enrollment dates, and eligibility rules for Marketplace coverage have changed in recent years and can change again. Treat this article as a guide to how things work, and confirm current figures before you decide.

How the subsidy is calculated

When you apply for a Marketplace plan, you estimate your household income for the coming year. Based on that estimate, your household size, and local plan prices, the Marketplace determines whether you qualify for a premium tax credit and how much. You can take the credit in advance, lowering your monthly premium, or claim it all at tax time.

The amount is not locked in. It is an estimate of what you should receive.

What happens at tax time

When you file your tax return, you reconcile the advance credit you received with the credit you were actually entitled to based on your real income. The result can go in either direction:

  • If your actual income was higher than estimated, you may have received too much credit and may need to repay some of it. Repayment rules depend on current law and have changed in recent years, so confirm the current rules.
  • If your actual income was lower, you may qualify for a bigger credit and receive the difference as a refund or reduced tax.
Do not guess. If your income is hard to predict, it can help to estimate carefully, update during the year, and talk to a tax professional about the reconciliation process.

Why updating during the year helps

Reporting changes lets the Marketplace adjust your monthly credit closer to reality. That reduces the risk of a big repayment, or lets you capture more help sooner if your income dropped. You may want to update when:

  • You change jobs or get a raise or bonus.
  • You lose your job or work fewer hours.
  • Your business income looks very different from your forecast.
  • A household member joins or leaves.
  • You start receiving unemployment, Social Security, or other income.

Special situations

  • Self-employed. Your income may vary month to month. Build your estimate from recent history and expected contracts. See self-employed health insurance options.
  • Job loss. If you lost your job, your estimate for the year should reflect wages before the loss, any severance, and unemployment benefits. Learn about options after losing job coverage.
  • Marriage or divorce. Household size and filing status change the calculation.
  • Medicaid eligibility. A significant drop in income may make you eligible for Medicaid in some states, which has its own rules.

A practical approach to estimating

  1. Start with what you earned last year and adjust for known changes.
  2. Add expected bonuses, side income, and investment income.
  3. Subtract only deductions that apply to the income calculation used by the Marketplace.
  4. If your income is uncertain, consider planning for the middle of your range and update as the year unfolds.
  5. Keep documentation of your income changes.

Choosing a plan when income is uncertain

If repaying a subsidy would cause hardship, you can choose to take less of the credit in advance and claim the rest at tax time. This reduces the chance of owing but lowers your monthly savings. Talk through the trade-off with an advisor.

Keep good records

Whatever happens to your income, keep copies of pay stubs, invoices, unemployment statements, and the Marketplace notices you receive. At tax time, you will receive a form from the Marketplace showing the advance credits paid on your behalf, and your tax preparer will need it. Having your income records organized makes reconciliation simpler and helps you catch mistakes before they cost you.

When to call before changing anything

If you are thinking of taking on a big contract, quitting a job, or selling an asset, a quick conversation first can help you see how it might affect your subsidy. It is easier to plan around a change than to unwind it after the fact.

Questions worth asking your tax preparer

Ask how advance credits will be reconciled on your return, whether any repayment limits apply under current law, and how self-employment deductions affect the income figure used for the credit. Bringing these questions early, not in April, gives you time to adjust your estimate and your monthly credit before the year ends.

Common questions

ACA Subsidy & Income Changes FAQ

Do I have to report income changes to the Marketplace?
You should report significant changes in household income or size so your advance premium tax credit can be adjusted. Doing so can reduce surprises at tax time.
What happens if I earned more than I estimated?
When you file your tax return, you reconcile the credits you received against what you were actually eligible for. Depending on the rules in effect, you may owe some or all of the excess back.
What if I earned less than I estimated?
You may be eligible for a larger credit, which can come as a refund or reduced tax when you reconcile, and you may qualify for more help during the year by updating your application.
