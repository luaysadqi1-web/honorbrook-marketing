TSP vs. IRA after retirement: fees, fund choices and withdrawal flexibility compared

Neither is automatically better. The TSP is a simple, low-cost plan with a short fund menu, while an IRA offers far more investment and withdrawal choices at a cost that depends on the provider. Your best fit depends on fees, how much choice you want and how you plan to take income.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Once you separate from federal service or the military, you do not have to move your Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) account. Per tsp.gov, you can generally leave it there if your vested balance is $200 or more. So the real question is whether moving it to an IRA gets you something worth having. This guide compares the two on the points that matter. It is general information; the TSP's own rules always control.

The short version
  • TSP: a small menu of funds, published low expenses, and withdrawal options set by the plan.
  • IRA: nearly unlimited investment choices and flexible withdrawals, but fees vary widely by provider and product.
  • You can split the difference by moving part of your balance and leaving the rest in the TSP.

Fees: where the TSP is hard to beat, and where an IRA can compete

The TSP publishes its fund and administrative expenses at tsp.gov, and its core funds are index-style. An IRA has no single fee. You might pay fund expense ratios, account fees, trading costs or an advisory fee, or in the case of insurance products, rider charges and surrender charges. Ask any IRA provider for every annual cost in writing, then compare the total to the TSP's published figures. We do not quote specific percentages here because the TSP updates them each year.

Fund choices: simple menu vs. open shelf

The TSP offers a handful of individual funds plus target-date style lifecycle funds, which suits people who want a short, easy-to-manage list. An IRA can hold stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs and, at some custodians, annuities. More choice means more ways to build a tailored plan, and also more ways to overpay or take on risk you did not intend.

  • Choose the TSP if you value simplicity and are comfortable with its fund menu.
  • Choose an IRA if you want investments or income products the TSP does not offer, or want to combine accounts you already have.
  • Consider both if you want a low-cost core in the TSP and a more customized slice elsewhere.

Withdrawal flexibility after you retire

Per tsp.gov, separated participants can take a partial or total withdrawal, set up installment payments, or buy an annuity through the plan. An IRA is generally more flexible: you decide how much to take and when, subject to tax rules and any limits the provider sets. That flexibility is a benefit if your income needs change year to year, and a risk if it tempts you to withdraw too much too soon.

Taxes and required minimum distributions

Traditional balances in either account are generally taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, and a direct rollover is generally not taxable by itself. Required minimum distributions apply to both under IRS rules, and an RMD itself cannot be rolled over. Moving money between traditional and Roth can create a tax bill, so check with a tax professional first. If you do move money, a direct rollover avoids the withholding problems of an indirect one. See our guide to rolling over your TSP for the 60-day rule.

Where an annuity fits in the comparison

Some retirees want part of their savings to produce income they cannot outlive. The TSP offers an annuity option, and an IRA can hold an annuity from an insurance company. Annuities are not investments in the stock market, may carry surrender charges, fees and limits, and guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability. See what to ask before an IRA rollover into an annuity and our annuities page.

Questions to ask before you decide

  • What is the total annual cost of each option, in writing?
  • Which features of the TSP would I give up, and do I actually use them?
  • How will I take income, and which account makes that easier?
  • Who is paid if I move the money, and how?

Common questions

TSP vs. IRA FAQ

Is the TSP cheaper than an IRA?
The TSP publishes its expenses at tsp.gov, and its index-style funds are generally low-cost. An IRA's cost depends entirely on the provider and what you buy, so it can be lower or higher. Compare the total annual cost of each option in writing, including advisory and product fees.
Can I split my money between the TSP and an IRA?
Yes. Separated participants can generally take a partial withdrawal or partial rollover and leave the rest in the TSP. Confirm the current rules on tsp.gov before you start, because transfer steps and tax treatment depend on how you request the money.
Do TSP and IRA withdrawals work the same way for taxes?
Traditional balances in either account are generally taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, and required minimum distributions apply to both under IRS rules. Roth balances follow their own rules. A tax professional can tell you how your own situation works.
