Medicare in Dallas County

Dallas County includes Dallas, the county seat, and neighboring communities across the county. The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2023 population estimates count about 317,636 residents age 65 and older in the county (source: U.S. Census Bureau, 2023 population estimates).

Dallas County’s public hospital system is Parkland Health, run by the Dallas County Hospital District and the main teaching hospital for UT Southwestern Medical Center. Baylor University Medical Center, part of Baylor Scott & White Health, is also located in Dallas.

Medicare Advantage and Part D plan availability and provider networks are set county by county, so the plans open to you in Dallas County may differ from those in a neighboring county. We check the options for your ZIP code, your doctors and your prescriptions before you decide. We are not based in Dallas County; we help Dallas County residents by phone or video call.

Among Texas’s 254 counties, Dallas County comes in 2nd for residents age 65 and up (317,636). Older adults make up 12.2% of the population here; the state figure is 13.7%. The 139,179 residents now between 60 and 64 will become Medicare-eligible soon, and our turning 65 guide walks through it. Among residents 65 and older, 62% are 65 to 74, 29% are 75 to 84 and 9% are 85 or older (Census 2023 estimates). CMS data show 55.0% of Dallas County residents with Medicare chose Medicare Advantage, which is close to the 54.9% average across Texas. What most people do in a county is less important than what fits you. We weigh Medicare Advantage against Original Medicare with Medigap using your doctors, your drugs and your budget.

Who has Medicare in Dallas County

The U.S. Department of Agriculture classifies Dallas County as a metro county (counties in metro areas of 1 million population or more). Counties like this tend to have a wider choice of hospitals and Medicare plans than rural ones, though networks still differ plan by plan.

CMS counts 359,395 Medicare beneficiaries in Dallas County (2023). Their average age is 73, about the same as the Texas average of 73. About 11% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, close to the state figure of 11%.

Among people in Original Medicare in Dallas County, CMS reported about 569 emergency room visits and 233 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (580) and close to it for hospital stays (226). About 11% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Dallas County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Dallas County by phone?
Yes. We are an independent agency licensed in Texas, and we help Dallas County residents by phone or video call, so you can compare plans from home. There is no pressure to enroll.
Will my Dallas County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Parkland Health or Baylor University Medical Center, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
Do you charge Dallas County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
