Mortgage protection insurance: keep your family in the house

If you died tomorrow, could your family keep making the mortgage payment? Mortgage protection is life insurance sized to your home loan. It pays your family directly, so they decide whether to pay off the house, keep making payments, or move.

What mortgage protection insurance is

Mortgage protection is not a special product. It is term life insurance chosen to match your mortgage: the coverage amount is close to your loan balance, and the term runs about as long as the loan. A 30-year mortgage is usually paired with a 30-year term, a 15-year refinance with a 15-year term.

You own the policy and you name the beneficiary. If you die during the term, your family receives the death benefit, generally free of federal income tax, and can use it however they need.

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is often confused with mortgage protection. PMI is something you pay for that protects the lender. It does nothing for your family if you die.

What you can add

  • Living benefits. Many policies let you draw on part of the death benefit early if you are diagnosed with a terminal, chronic, or critical illness. Terms vary by carrier and state.
  • Disability or waiver riders. Some carriers waive premiums, or pay a monthly amount, if you become disabled.
  • Return of premium. Costs more, and refunds your premiums if you outlive the term.
  • No-exam underwriting. Many applicants qualify with health questions and a records check instead of a medical exam.
Those letters in your mailbox: After a home purchase or refinance, most people get mail that looks like it came from their lender offering mortgage protection. It is marketing, usually from an insurance agency. You are free to compare. The right policy is the one that fits your health, budget, and loan, whoever mailed you first.

Who should look at it

  • New homeowners and anyone who just refinanced into a larger loan
  • Households that need both incomes to make the payment
  • Single parents and anyone with a co-signer
  • Self-employed owners whose income would stop if they did

If you already carry enough life insurance to pay off the house and replace your income, you may not need a separate policy. We will tell you so. See our term life guide and the life insurance calculator.

Common questions

Mortgage Protection Insurance FAQ

Is mortgage protection insurance the same as PMI?
No. PMI protects the lender if you stop paying. Mortgage protection is life insurance that pays your family if you die, so they can keep the home.
Do I need a medical exam?
Often not. Many carriers approve mortgage protection with health questions and a records check. A full exam can still be the lower-cost route for some people, and we compare both.
What happens if I refinance, move, or pay the house off early?
The policy belongs to you, not the loan. It continues as long as you pay the premium, and you can keep it, reduce it, or cancel it.
How much coverage should I buy?
Most people start with the loan balance, then add enough to cover a few years of taxes, insurance, and living costs. We size it with you.
