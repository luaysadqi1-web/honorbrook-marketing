Medicare in Kent County

Kent County includes Grand Rapids and the communities around it in West Michigan. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, 2023 population estimates, about 102,130 Kent County residents are age 65 or older, so choosing the right Medicare coverage is a decision many of your neighbors are making too. Whether you are turning 65, retiring, or reviewing your current plan, we can walk through your choices with you on a call at a time that suits you.

Major hospitals and health systems serving the county include Corewell Health Butterworth Hospital and Trinity Health Grand Rapids Hospital, both in Grand Rapids. Medicare Advantage and Part D plan availability and provider networks are set county by county, and they can change every year. Original Medicare (Parts A and B) is the same nationwide, but the private plans you can add to it are not. That is why we check the plans offered where you live and confirm that your doctors and pharmacy are included before you enroll.

With roughly 102,130 residents age 65 and older, Kent County is 4th among 83 Michigan counties for older residents. That group is 15.4% of its population, compared with 19.2% across Michigan. Another 39,046 residents are age 60 to 64 and will reach Medicare eligibility within about five years (see our turning 65 guide). By age, the 65-and-older group splits 61% ages 65 to 74, 29% ages 75 to 84 and 10% age 85 and up (Census 2023). Medicare Advantage enrollment in Kent County is 74.5% of people with Medicare, higher than the Michigan average of 63.6%. What most people do in a county is less important than what fits you. We weigh Medicare Advantage against Original Medicare with Medigap using your doctors, your drugs and your budget.

Who has Medicare in Kent County

Kent County sits in a metro area, which the USDA defines as counties in metro areas of 1 million population or more. That usually means more doctors, more hospitals and more plan choices in the area.

CMS counts 122,709 Medicare beneficiaries in Kent County (2023). Their average age is 70, younger than the Michigan average of 72. About 25% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, higher than the state figure of 20%. Where many people have both, special plans for dual-eligible members can matter; see our Medicaid and dual eligible guide, since availability varies by plan and area.

Among people in Original Medicare in Kent County, CMS reported about 677 emergency room visits and 216 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (645) and close to it for hospital stays (245). About 9% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Kent County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Kent County by phone?
Yes. We are an independent agency licensed in Michigan, and we help Kent County residents by phone or video call. We review your doctors, prescriptions and budget, then walk you through the options available at your ZIP code.
Will my Kent County doctors and Corewell Health Butterworth or Trinity Health Grand Rapids be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan's network, so before you enroll we check whether your specific doctors and the hospitals you use are in network. Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare.
Do you charge Kent County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers compensate us when you enroll, and that does not change your premium.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event. We can tell you which applies to you.
