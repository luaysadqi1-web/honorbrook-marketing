With roughly 33,437 residents age 65 and older, Muskegon County is 14th among 83 Michigan counties for older residents. That group is 18.9% of its population, compared with 19.2% across Michigan. Another 12,369 residents are age 60 to 64 and will reach Medicare eligibility within about five years (see our turning 65 guide). Among residents 65 and older, 61% are 65 to 74, 30% are 75 to 84 and 9% are 85 or older (Census 2023 estimates). Medicare Advantage enrollment in Muskegon County is 75.4% of people with Medicare, higher than the Michigan average of 63.6%. Averages cannot choose a plan for you. Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare with a Medigap plan each suit different people, depending on doctors, medicines and how you prefer to handle costs, and we compare both at your ZIP code.

Who has Medicare in Muskegon County

Muskegon County is a metro county; the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes it as: counties in metro areas of fewer than 250,000 population. Metro counties usually have more hospitals and more insurers competing for Medicare members, so there can be more plans to compare.

CMS counts 43,167 Medicare beneficiaries in Muskegon County (2023). Their average age is 70, younger than the Michigan average of 72. About 28% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, higher than the state figure of 20%. Where many people have both, special plans for dual-eligible members can matter; see our Medicaid and dual eligible guide, since availability varies by plan and area.

Among people in Original Medicare in Muskegon County, CMS reported about 702 emergency room visits and 238 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (645) and close to it for hospital stays (245). About 8% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Insurers choose which counties to serve each year, which means the Medicare Advantage and Part D plans you can pick in Muskegon County can be different from the ones offered next door. We are not based in Muskegon County; we help Muskegon County residents by phone or video call.

Where to get free Medicare help in Muskegon County

  • MMAP, the Michigan state health insurance assistance program (SHIP), offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling: 1-800-803-7174.
  • To reach your local Area Agency on Aging, use the Eldercare Locator or call 1-800-677-1116.
  • 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) is the federal Medicare helpline.

Start with any of these if you like. We pick up where they leave off: comparing the actual plans for your doctors and drugs, and staying with you each year at renewal.

Common questions

Muskegon County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Muskegon County by phone?
Yes. Phone and video appointments are how we serve Muskegon County. We hold a Michigan license, compare plans from many carriers and walk you through them from home, with no pressure to enroll.
Will my Muskegon County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Trinity Health Muskegon Hospital, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Muskegon County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 32,976 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 43,720 people with Medicare in Muskegon County in September 2026, or 75.4%. The Michigan average was 63.6%.
Do you charge Muskegon County residents for help?
No, there is no fee. The insurance company pays us when you enroll, and your premium does not go up because you used an agent.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
