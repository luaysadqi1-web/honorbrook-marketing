Rolling Retirement Money Into an Annuity: Every Account Type, What Is Allowed and the Traps

Most retirement accounts can be moved into an annuity, usually by rolling them to an IRA first, but not all of them, and each type has its own trap. A non-governmental 457(b) cannot be rolled over at all, a SIMPLE IRA is locked for two years, and a check made out to you from a plan triggers 20% withholding.

By Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent (NPN 21370662) · October 2026

Important: this article is educational, not personalized advice. Annuity guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability, and annuities can carry surrender charges and fees. They are not bank products or investments. Speak with a licensed professional and your tax advisor before moving money.

Which accounts can fund an annuity?

An annuity is bought with money that is already in an IRA or a plan, or with money from another annuity. The question is whether the account you have can legally move there without tax. This table is based on the IRS rollover chart and the IRS pages linked below it.

Rules that apply to every rollover

  • Direct is safer than a check. Ask the plan or IRA to send the money straight to the new institution. A plan distribution paid to you is subject to mandatory 20% withholding even if you plan to roll it over, and to roll over the full amount you must replace the withheld money from other funds (IRS Topic 413).
  • The 60-day clock. If you receive the money yourself, you generally have 60 days to deposit it in another plan or IRA. The IRS can waive the deadline in some cases where circumstances were beyond your control (IRS: Rollovers of retirement plan and IRA distributions).
  • One IRA rollover per 12 months. The limit applies across all your IRAs together, including SEP, SIMPLE, traditional and Roth. It does not apply to trustee-to-trustee transfers, Roth conversions, or rollovers between a plan and an IRA.
  • Required minimum distributions cannot be rolled over. The IRS lists age 73 as the starting age on its current page, and your own date depends on your birth year, so confirm yours (IRS: required minimum distributions).
  • Report it. A rollover is not taxable when done correctly, but it is reportable on your federal tax return.

What an annuity changes, and what it does not

An IRA already defers taxes, so an annuity inside an IRA does not add tax deferral. What an annuity adds is a contract: possible income guarantees, protection features, or a way to turn savings into payments. What it costs is liquidity: many contracts carry surrender charges if you withdraw early, and the guarantees depend on the issuing company’s claims-paying ability. Which situation sounds like yours?

  • You just left a job and have an old 401(k): your 401(k) options.
  • You work for a school, hospital or nonprofit: 403(b) or 457(b).
  • You own a small business: SEP and SIMPLE IRAs.
  • You were offered a pension buyout: lump sum or monthly.
  • You are 55 to 59½ and may need cash: the age 55 rule.
  • You are approaching required withdrawals: QLACs and RMDs.
  • You already own an annuity: the 1035 exchange.
  • You inherited an IRA: spouse and non-spouse rules.
  • You have Roth money: Roth rollover rules.

Questions to ask before any rollover

  • Is this a direct rollover, and will the money go straight to the new institution?
  • What surrender charges apply, for how long, and what can I take out each year without a charge?
  • What are all the fees, including rider fees, and who is paid a commission?
  • What will I give up by leaving my current account, such as plan features, exceptions to the 10% additional tax, or a lower-cost option?
  • Does the contract’s start date or payout schedule fit when I will actually need the money?
  • Who is the issuing company, and what is its financial strength rating from independent agencies?
  • What does my tax professional say about the tax result?

Take your time

A reputable carrier gives you a free-look period after you receive the contract, during which you can cancel and get your money back under the contract terms and state law. Use it to read the entire contract and share it with your tax professional. If anyone says an offer disappears unless you sign today, that is a reason to slow down.

Common questions

Retirement Rollover FAQ

Which retirement accounts can be rolled into an annuity?
Traditional, SEP and (after two years) SIMPLE IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, governmental 457(b)s and pension lump sums can generally be rolled to an IRA, which can then hold an annuity. A non-governmental 457(b) cannot be rolled over, and a Roth IRA can move only to another Roth IRA.
Is rolling a 401(k) into an annuity taxable?
A direct rollover to a traditional IRA is generally not taxable when done correctly. A distribution paid to you can have 20% withheld, and money you do not deposit within 60 days is generally taxable. Moving pre-tax money to a Roth IRA is taxable as income.
Does an annuity in an IRA give extra tax benefits?
No. The IRA already defers tax, so the reason to use an annuity is its contract features, such as income options, not extra tax deferral.
Is a 1035 exchange the same as a rollover?
No. A 1035 exchange moves one annuity into another and must go directly between insurers. A rollover moves money between retirement accounts.
