Medicare and Medicaid? You have options most people never hear about

What "dual eligible" actually means

Dual eligible means you qualify for Medicare and Medicaid at the same time. When that happens, Medicare becomes your primary coverage and Medicaid steps in to help with what Medicare doesn't fully cover — which can include your premiums, deductibles, copays, and services Medicare largely leaves out, such as long-term care.

Millions of Americans are dual eligible, and a striking number don't know it. Qualifying for Medicaid doesn't come with a letter explaining which Medicare plans that unlocks, and qualifying for Medicare doesn't prompt anyone to check whether you'd also qualify for Medicaid. The two programs are run by different agencies, and the gap between them is where people lose benefits they're entitled to.

D-SNP plans: built for this exact situation

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a type of Medicare Advantage plan designed only for people with both Medicare and Medicaid. Instead of managing two programs that don't talk to each other, a D-SNP coordinates both under one plan and one card.

Because Medicaid is already covering much of your cost sharing, D-SNPs can direct their budget toward benefits traditional Medicare doesn't offer. Depending on the plan and your county, that commonly includes:

  • Dental, vision, and hearing coverage — the three things people are most surprised Original Medicare doesn't include.
  • Transportation to and from medical appointments.
  • An over-the-counter or grocery allowance — a set amount each month or quarter for approved items.
  • Care coordination — a case manager who helps organize your providers and benefits.
  • $0 or very low plan premiums, on top of the Medicare costs Medicaid is already helping with.

Benefits vary significantly by plan, carrier, and county — this is not a product where one plan is best for everyone, and that's precisely why comparing them matters.

An important Virginia-specific rule: if you enroll in a D-SNP in Virginia, you generally must also be enrolled in the Medicaid managed care plan run by that same insurance company. The alignment is deliberate — it's what lets one organization coordinate both sides of your coverage. But it means choosing a D-SNP also chooses your Medicaid plan, so it's worth getting right the first time.

Who qualifies for Medicaid in Virginia

Virginia expanded Medicaid, and the program is administered under the name Cardinal Care. In broad terms:

  • Adults under 65 generally qualify with household income up to 138% of the federal poverty level. Eligibility at this level is based on income, not assets.
  • Adults 65 and older, and people who are blind or have a disability, fall under different rules that consider both income and assets, with separate limits.
  • Long-term care Medicaid — coverage for nursing facility care or home and community-based services — has its own eligibility standards, generally stricter and asset-tested.

Income and asset limits are adjusted annually, so any specific dollar figure you read online goes stale quickly. The determination is made by the Commonwealth, not by an insurance agency — you apply through CoverVA at 1-855-242-8282 or at coverva.dmas.virginia.gov. Extra Help and Medicare Savings Programs — check even if Medicaid says no

This is the part we most often find people missing. There are programs that help with Medicare costs at income levels above full Medicaid eligibility, and they're chronically underused.

Extra Help (Low Income Subsidy)Part D prescription drug premiums, deductibles, and copaysPrescription costs are a strain, even modestly

Qualifying for a Medicare Savings Program often also qualifies you automatically for Extra Help, and either can open the door to a D-SNP. If someone once told you that you "make too much for Medicaid," that answer says nothing about these programs. It's worth a second look.

If you don't qualify for Medicaid

Not qualifying isn't a dead end, it just changes the path. If you're under 65 and above the Medicaid threshold, an ACA marketplace plan with premium tax credits is usually the next step, and those subsidies often cut the cost dramatically — many people who assume marketplace coverage is unaffordable have never seen their actual subsidized price. Our blog post on choosing between ACA and Medicaid in Virginia walks through where the line falls.

If you're on Medicare and not dual eligible, then a standard Medicare Advantage plan or a Medicare Supplement paired with a Part D plan is where to look. Either way, the comparison is free and there's no obligation.

Enrollment timing for dual eligibles

One genuine advantage of dual eligibility: you generally aren't locked into the standard Medicare calendar. Most people can only change Medicare Advantage plans during the Annual Enrollment Period each October 15 – December 7. People with both Medicare and Medicaid typically have a Special Enrollment Period that allows plan changes more often during the year — commonly once per quarter for the first three quarters, though the specific rules depend on your situation and level of Medicaid eligibility.

That flexibility matters. If you're in a plan that isn't working — your doctor left the network, your medication isn't covered well — you may not have to wait until fall to fix it.

Medicaid & Dual Eligible FAQ

What does "dual eligible" mean?
You qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid at the same time. Medicare becomes your primary coverage and Medicaid helps with what Medicare doesn't fully cover — premiums, deductibles, copays, and services like long-term care. Millions of Americans are dual eligible and many don't realize it.
What is a D-SNP plan?
A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan is a Medicare Advantage plan built specifically for people with both Medicare and Medicaid. It coordinates both programs in one plan and often includes dental, vision, hearing, transportation, and an over-the-counter or grocery benefit — typically at no additional premium.
Who qualifies for Medicaid in Virginia?
Virginia expanded Medicaid, so adults under 65 generally qualify with household income up to 138% of the federal poverty level. Separate rules apply for adults 65+ and people who are blind or have disabilities, considering assets as well as income. Limits change annually, and the Commonwealth makes the determination — apply through CoverVA at 1-855-242-8282.
Do I have to take a Medicaid plan to join a D-SNP in Virginia?
In Virginia, D-SNP members generally must also enroll in the Medicaid managed care plan run by that same insurance company. The alignment is intentional so one organization coordinates both sides. It matters when comparing plans, because your D-SNP choice also determines your Medicaid plan.
What are Extra Help and Medicare Savings Programs?
Extra Help (the Low Income Subsidy) reduces Part D drug costs — premiums, deductibles, copays. Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB, QI) help pay Medicare premiums and sometimes deductibles and coinsurance. Many people qualify for these without qualifying for full Medicaid, and most who qualify never apply.
What if I don't qualify for Medicaid?
If you're under 65 and above the limit, an ACA marketplace plan with premium tax credits is usually next, and subsidies often reduce the cost substantially. If you're on Medicare, a standard Medicare Advantage plan or a Supplement with Part D may fit better. We help either way, free.
Can I change plans outside of fall enrollment?
Often yes. People with both Medicare and Medicaid typically have a Special Enrollment Period allowing plan changes more often than the standard October 15 – December 7 window — commonly once per quarter for the first three quarters, depending on your situation and level of Medicaid eligibility.
