What is Medicare Advantage (Part C)?

Medicare Advantage — also called Part C — is a way to get your Medicare benefits through a private insurance company approved by Medicare instead of through Original Medicare alone. When you enroll in an Advantage plan, the plan provides your Part A (hospital) and Part B (medical) coverage, and almost always bundles in extras Original Medicare doesn't cover.

Most Advantage plans roll your hospital, medical, and prescription drug coverage into a single plan with one card — and many add benefits like dental, vision, hearing aids, fitness memberships, and over-the-counter allowances. A large share of plans carry a $0 monthly premium (you still pay your Part B premium), which is why they're the most popular choice for new enrollees.

The honest tradeoff: $0-premium plans aren't "free" — you trade lower monthly cost for provider networks and copays when you use care. The right plan is the one whose network, drug list, and out-of-pocket maximum fit your doctors, prescriptions, and budget. That's exactly what we check for you, at no cost.

What's included in a Medicare Advantage plan?

  • Part A & Part B — your hospital and medical coverage, administered by the plan.
  • Prescription drugs (Part D) — built into most Advantage plans, so you don't buy a separate drug plan.
  • An annual out-of-pocket maximum — a yearly cap on what you'll pay for covered medical services, something Original Medicare alone doesn't have.
  • Extra benefits — many plans include dental, vision, hearing, fitness (like SilverSneakers), and OTC allowances. These vary plan to plan.

Who is Medicare Advantage a good fit for?

Advantage plans tend to fit people who want predictable, low monthly costs, like the convenience of bundled benefits, are comfortable using a network of local providers, and want a built-in cap on annual medical spending. If you travel often, see specialists outside your area, or want to keep any doctor nationwide, a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan may suit you better — and we're happy to compare both.

When can you enroll in or switch a Medicare Advantage plan?

  • Initial Enrollment Period (IEP): the 7-month window around your 65th birthday.
  • Annual Enrollment Period (AEP): October 15 – December 7 each year — change plans for the following year.
  • Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period: January 1 – March 31 — if you're already in an Advantage plan, you can switch once.
  • Special Enrollment Periods (SEP): triggered by moving, losing coverage, qualifying for Extra Help, and other life events — these open year-round.

Common questions

Medicare Advantage FAQ

How much does a Medicare Advantage plan cost?
Many Advantage plans have a $0 monthly premium, though you still pay your Part B premium. Your real costs come from copays, coinsurance, and the plan's annual out-of-pocket maximum. We compare total expected cost — not just the premium — so you see the true price.
Will my doctors and prescriptions be covered?
Advantage plans use provider networks and drug formularies that change each year. Before you enroll, we verify your doctors are in network and run your medication list through each plan to confirm coverage and cost.
When can I enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan?
During your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7), during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (Jan 1 – Mar 31), or during a Special Enrollment Period triggered by a qualifying life event.
Is Medicare Advantage or Medigap better?
Neither is universally better. Advantage bundles benefits with low or $0 premiums but uses networks and copays. Medigap costs more monthly but lets you see any doctor who accepts Medicare with minimal out-of-pocket costs. It depends on your health, budget, doctors, and travel — and we'll weigh both with you.
