Gaines County ranks 159th of 254 Texas counties by number of residents age 65 and older, with about 2,081 people in that age group, or 9.2% of residents (statewide: 13.7%). Another 1,071 residents are age 60 to 64 and will reach Medicare eligibility within about five years (see our turning 65 guide). Among residents 65 and older, 61% are 65 to 74, 27% are 75 to 84 and 11% are 85 or older (Census 2023 estimates). CMS data show 37.5% of Gaines County residents with Medicare chose Medicare Advantage, which is lower than the 54.9% average across Texas. A county average does not tell you which kind of plan fits you. Whether Medicare Advantage or Original Medicare with a Medigap plan is the better fit depends on your doctors, your prescriptions and how you want to handle costs, so we compare both for your ZIP code.

Who has Medicare in Gaines County

Gaines County is a smaller-city and town county; the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes it as: urban population of 5,000 to 20,000, adjacent to a metro area. Counties like this often sit between a metro area and rural communities, so some residents use hospitals and specialists in a neighboring county and a plan’s network across the county line matters.

CMS counts 2,222 Medicare beneficiaries in Gaines County (2023). Their average age is 73, about the same as the Texas average of 73. About 21% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, higher than the state figure of 11%. Where many people have both, special plans for dual-eligible members can matter; see our Medicaid and dual eligible guide, since availability varies by plan and area.

Among people in Original Medicare in Gaines County, CMS reported about 691 emergency room visits and 207 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is higher than the state average for emergency visits (580) and close to it for hospital stays (226). About 10% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Where to get free Medicare help in Gaines County

  • HICAP, the Texas state health insurance assistance program (SHIP), offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling: 1-800-252-9240.
  • To reach your local Area Agency on Aging, use the Eldercare Locator or call 1-800-677-1116.
  • The federal Medicare helpline is 1-800-MEDICARE, 1-800-633-4227.

Start with any of these if you like. We pick up where they leave off: comparing the actual plans for your doctors and drugs, and staying with you each year at renewal.

Common questions

Gaines County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Gaines County by phone?
Yes. We are an independent agency licensed in Texas, and we help Gaines County residents by phone or video call, so you can compare plans from home. There is no pressure to enroll.
Will my Gaines County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Memorial Hospital, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Gaines County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 874 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 2,333 people with Medicare in Gaines County in September 2026, or 37.5%. The Texas average was 54.9%.
Do you charge Gaines County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
