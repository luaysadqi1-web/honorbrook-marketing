The five types of life insurance

Life insurance comes in five main shapes, and the best one for you depends on what you're trying to protect and for how long. Here's the honest, plain-English version of each — with a dedicated page for every one if you want to go deeper.

  • Term life — the most affordable option and the right answer for most families. It covers you for a set period (commonly 10, 20, or 30 years) and pays a benefit if you pass away during that term. It's the workhorse for income replacement during your working and mortgage years — when your family would feel the loss of your paycheck the most. When the term ends, so does the coverage, which is exactly why it costs less.
  • Whole life — lifelong coverage that never expires as long as you pay the premium. Premiums stay level, and the policy builds guaranteed cash value over time that you can borrow against. You pay more than term for the same death benefit, but you're buying permanence and an accumulating asset, not just temporary protection.
  • Indexed universal life (IUL) — permanent coverage whose cash value growth is linked to a market index, with a cap on the upside and a floor protecting against index losses. More growth potential than whole life, fewer guarantees, and it demands consistent funding and regular review. Heavily marketed and frequently misunderstood — we cover the honest tradeoffs in detail.
  • Guaranteed universal life (GUL) — permanent coverage stripped to essentials. It guarantees a death benefit to a set age at far less cost than whole life, because it builds little or no cash value by design. The best value per dollar if what you need is a guaranteed benefit and nothing else.
  • Final expense — a small whole-life policy designed to cover funeral, burial, and other final costs rather than replace income. Coverage amounts are modest, approval is usually easy, and it's a practical fit for seniors who simply don't want to leave those bills behind.

Start here if you're not sure: ask yourself one question — does the need ever end? If your family would be fine once the mortgage is paid and the kids are grown, you want term, and you want as much of it as your budget allows. If the need genuinely never ends — estate taxes, a lifelong dependent, a business obligation — you're choosing among the permanent options, and the next question is whether you value guarantees (whole, GUL) or growth potential (IUL).

How much life insurance do you need?

The right amount comes down to what your family would need if your income disappeared. Think in terms of replacing your income for the years your household would rely on it, paying off your mortgage and other debts, covering your children's living and education costs, and handling final expenses. A common rule of thumb is roughly 10 times your annual income, but that's a starting point, not a verdict — we help you size it to your real numbers.

A simple way to start: add up your mortgage and debts, plus 10 years of income, plus what it would cost to raise and educate your kids, plus final expenses — then subtract savings you'd leave behind. The result is a realistic coverage target. We'll walk through it with you, free of charge.

No-exam and simplified options

You don't always need a needle and a paramedic visit to get covered. Many carriers offer no-exam and simplified-issue policies where you answer a handful of health questions and skip the medical exam entirely. Approval can be fast — sometimes within days — which is ideal if you want coverage in place quickly or would rather not schedule an exam. Fully underwritten policies still tend to offer the lowest rates for healthy applicants, so we'll show you both paths and let you choose.

Common questions

Life Insurance FAQ

Term or whole life — which is better?
Neither is universally better — it depends on your goals and budget. Term is cheaper and ideal for temporary needs like replacing income or covering a mortgage. Whole life is permanent and builds cash value, but costs more. We help you weigh both against what you're protecting.
Do I need a medical exam?
Often no. Many carriers offer no-exam and simplified-issue policies where you answer a few health questions instead of taking an exam. These can be approved quickly, and we'll match you to the option that fits your situation.
How much coverage do I need?
It depends on your income, debts, dependents, and final costs. A common rule of thumb is about 10 times your annual income, plus enough to cover your mortgage and your children's needs. We help you calculate a realistic number rather than guess.
Can I get covered with health conditions?
Usually yes. Carriers underwrite differently, so a condition that's a problem with one company may be fine with another. Because we're independent and compare many carriers, we can match you to the one most likely to approve you at the best rate.
