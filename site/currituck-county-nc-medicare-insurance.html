With roughly 5,722 residents age 65 and older, Currituck County is 75th among 100 North Carolina counties for older residents. That group is 18.1% of its population, compared with 17.6% across North Carolina. The 2,669 residents now between 60 and 64 will become Medicare-eligible soon, and our turning 65 guide walks through it. Census 2023 estimates show 65% of the 65-plus population is under 75, 28% is 75 to 84 and 6% is 85 or older. CMS data show 33.0% of Currituck County residents with Medicare chose Medicare Advantage, which is lower than the 57.9% average across North Carolina. A county average does not tell you which kind of plan fits you. Whether Medicare Advantage or Original Medicare with a Medigap plan is the better fit depends on your doctors, your prescriptions and how you want to handle costs, so we compare both for your ZIP code.

Who has Medicare in Currituck County

Currituck County is a metro county; the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes it as: counties in metro areas of 1 million population or more. Metro counties usually have more hospitals and more insurers competing for Medicare members, so there can be more plans to compare.

CMS counts 6,755 Medicare beneficiaries in Currituck County (2023). Their average age is 72, about the same as the North Carolina average of 73. About 8% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, lower than the state figure of 13%.

Among people in Original Medicare in Currituck County, CMS reported about 590 emergency room visits and 219 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (572) and close to it for hospital stays (210). About 7% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Hospitals and doctors near Currituck County

CMS Hospital General Information lists no acute care, critical access or children’s hospital located inside Currituck County. Residents often use hospitals in neighboring counties, which matters for Medicare Advantage because a plan’s network may include or leave out the hospital you use. Common questions

Currituck County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Currituck County by phone?
Yes. You do not need to travel: we are licensed in North Carolina and help Currituck County residents by phone or video, comparing plans from many insurers at no cost to you.
Will my Currituck County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use are in network, including hospitals in neighboring counties. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Currituck County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 2,420 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 7,328 people with Medicare in Currituck County in September 2026, or 33.0%. The North Carolina average was 57.9%.
Do you charge Currituck County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
