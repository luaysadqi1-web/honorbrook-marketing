Among Indiana’s 92 counties, Scott County comes in 71st for residents age 65 and up (4,243). Older adults make up 17.2% of the population here; the state figure is 17.2%. Another 1,783 residents are age 60 to 64 and will reach Medicare eligibility within about five years (see our turning 65 guide). About 58.5% of people with Medicare in Scott County are enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan. That is higher than the Indiana average of 51.1%. What most people do in a county is less important than what fits you. We weigh Medicare Advantage against Original Medicare with Medigap using your doctors, your drugs and your budget.

Who has Medicare in Scott County

Scott County is a smaller-city and town county; the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes it as: urban population of 5,000 to 20,000, adjacent to a metro area. Counties like this often sit between a metro area and rural communities, so some residents use hospitals and specialists in a neighboring county and a plan’s network across the county line matters.

CMS counts 5,928 Medicare beneficiaries in Scott County (2023). Their average age is 72, about the same as the Indiana average of 72. About 22% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, higher than the state figure of 16%. Where many people have both, special plans for dual-eligible members can matter; see our Medicaid and dual eligible guide, since availability varies by plan and area.

Among people in Original Medicare in Scott County, CMS reported about 684 emergency room visits and 244 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (619) and close to it for hospital stays (235). About 8% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Scott County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Scott County by phone?
Yes. Phone and video appointments are how we serve Scott County. We hold a Indiana license, compare plans from many carriers and walk you through them from home, with no pressure to enroll.
Will my Scott County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Norton Scott Hospital, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Scott County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 3,382 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 5,779 people with Medicare in Scott County in September 2026, or 58.5%. The Indiana average was 51.1%.
Do you charge Scott County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
