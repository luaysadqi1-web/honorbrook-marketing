Life insurance for high-net-worth families and high earners

At higher incomes and net worth, life insurance does different jobs: paying estate costs without a forced sale, keeping a business running, and treating heirs fairly. This page explains where it fits and where it does not.

Reviewed by Luay Sadqi, Licensed Agent · NPN 21370662 · Updated November 2026

What life insurance is used for at this level

  • Estate liquidity. Estates heavy in real estate or a private business can owe taxes and expenses in cash. A death benefit can pay them so heirs are not forced to sell.
  • Equalizing an inheritance. When one child takes over the business or the family property, insurance can give the others an equal share.
  • Business succession. Buy-sell agreements funded with life insurance give surviving owners the cash to buy out a deceased owner's family. Key-person coverage protects the company itself.
  • Income replacement for high earners. Physicians, executives, and owners often carry far less coverage than their income supports. See how much life insurance high earners need.
  • Charitable and legacy gifts. A policy can fund a gift far larger than the premiums paid.

State taxes many families overlook

The federal estate tax exemption is high, and most families never owe federal estate tax. State taxes are another matter. Several jurisdictions where we are licensed have their own estate or inheritance tax with far lower thresholds, including Maryland, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, and the District of Columbia. Rules and thresholds change, so confirm your exposure with an estate attorney or CPA.

What to know about IUL: An IUL is life insurance, not an investment in the market. Interest credits are limited by caps and participation rates the carrier can change. Policy charges rise with age, loans and withdrawals reduce the death benefit and can cause a policy to lapse, and surrender charges apply in the early years. Ownership matters as much as the policy

Who owns the policy affects whether the death benefit is counted in your estate. Many families use an irrevocable life insurance trust drafted by their attorney. We coordinate with your attorney and CPA rather than replace them.

How large cases are underwritten

Larger policies involve financial underwriting as well as medical: carriers want to see that the coverage amount matches your income, net worth, or business value. Expect an exam, medical records, and financial documentation. We prepare the file so the carrier sees a clear picture the first time.

Common questions

High-Net-Worth Life Insurance FAQ

Do I need life insurance if my estate is below the federal exemption?
Possibly. Some states, including Maryland, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, and the District of Columbia, have their own estate or inheritance taxes with lower thresholds, and insurance is also used for income replacement and business succession. An estate attorney or CPA can confirm your exposure.
Is indexed universal life a good fit for high earners?
It can be for people who need permanent coverage, have already used other savings vehicles, and can fund the policy well for many years. It carries caps, charges, and lapse risk, so it is not right for everyone. Term insurance plus regular investing is often the simpler answer.
What is a buy-sell agreement?
It is a contract among business owners that sets how an owner's share is bought out at death. Life insurance on each owner supplies the cash for the purchase.
Will you work with my attorney and CPA?
Yes. Ownership, trusts, and tax treatment are decided with your attorney and CPA. Our role is to design and place the insurance.
