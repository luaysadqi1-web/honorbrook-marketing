Marshall County ranks 16th of 67 Alabama counties by number of residents age 65 and older, with about 17,500 people in that age group, or 17.4% of residents (statewide: 18.3%). About 6,531 more people are 60 to 64 and are heading toward Medicare; our turning 65 guide explains their first steps. Census 2023 estimates show 59% of the 65-plus population is under 75, 32% is 75 to 84 and 9% is 85 or older. Medicare Advantage enrollment in Marshall County is 57.4% of people with Medicare, close to the Alabama average of 61.5%. What most people do in a county is less important than what fits you. We weigh Medicare Advantage against Original Medicare with Medigap using your doctors, your drugs and your budget.

Who has Medicare in Marshall County

The USDA classifies Marshall County as: urban population of 20,000 or more, adjacent to a metro area. In counties of this type, people often travel to a larger neighboring county for specialists, so we check whether your plan’s network reaches there.

CMS counts 22,604 Medicare beneficiaries in Marshall County (2023). Their average age is 73, about the same as the Alabama average of 72. About 16% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, close to the state figure of 14%.

Among people in Original Medicare in Marshall County, CMS reported about 569 emergency room visits and 209 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (600) and lower than it for hospital stays (247). About 11% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Marshall County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Marshall County by phone?
Yes. We are an independent agency licensed in Alabama, and we help Marshall County residents by phone or video call, so you can compare plans from home. There is no pressure to enroll.
Will my Marshall County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Marshall Medical Centers, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Marshall County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 12,633 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 22,014 people with Medicare in Marshall County in September 2026, or 57.4%. The Alabama average was 61.5%.
Do you charge Marshall County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
