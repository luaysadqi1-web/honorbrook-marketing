With roughly 9,655 residents age 65 and older, Watauga County is 60th among 100 North Carolina counties for older residents. That group is 17.6% of its population, compared with 17.6% across North Carolina. Another 3,170 residents are age 60 to 64 and will reach Medicare eligibility within about five years (see our turning 65 guide). By age, the 65-and-older group splits 61% ages 65 to 74, 30% ages 75 to 84 and 9% age 85 and up (Census 2023). About 46.9% of people with Medicare in Watauga County are enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan. That is lower than the North Carolina average of 57.9%. Averages cannot choose a plan for you. Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare with a Medigap plan each suit different people, depending on doctors, medicines and how you prefer to handle costs, and we compare both at your ZIP code.

Who has Medicare in Watauga County

Watauga County is a non-metro county with towns and small cities (USDA: urban population of 20,000 or more, not adjacent to a metro area). Care is often split between local providers and a bigger hospital elsewhere, which is worth testing against each plan’s network.

CMS counts 10,439 Medicare beneficiaries in Watauga County (2023). Their average age is 73, about the same as the North Carolina average of 73. About 9% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, lower than the state figure of 13%.

Among people in Original Medicare in Watauga County, CMS reported about 503 emergency room visits and 163 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is lower than the state average for emergency visits (572) and lower than it for hospital stays (210). About 8% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Watauga County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Watauga County by phone?
Yes. Phone and video appointments are how we serve Watauga County. We hold a North Carolina license, compare plans from many carriers and walk you through them from home, with no pressure to enroll.
Will my Watauga County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Watauga Medical Center, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Watauga County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 5,070 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 10,799 people with Medicare in Watauga County in September 2026, or 46.9%. The North Carolina average was 57.9%.
Do you charge Watauga County residents for help?
No. You pay nothing to work with us. Carriers compensate us, and the price of your plan is the same as if you enrolled directly.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
