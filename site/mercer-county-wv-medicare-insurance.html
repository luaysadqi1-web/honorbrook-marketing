With roughly 13,460 residents age 65 and older, Mercer County is 8th among 55 West Virginia counties for older residents. That group is 23.2% of its population, compared with 21.5% across West Virginia. The 3,697 residents now between 60 and 64 will become Medicare-eligible soon, and our turning 65 guide walks through it. Census 2023 estimates show 58% of the 65-plus population is under 75, 32% is 75 to 84 and 10% is 85 or older. CMS data show 59.5% of Mercer County residents with Medicare chose Medicare Advantage, which is close to the 56.6% average across West Virginia. A county average does not tell you which kind of plan fits you. Whether Medicare Advantage or Original Medicare with a Medigap plan is the better fit depends on your doctors, your prescriptions and how you want to handle costs, so we compare both for your ZIP code.

Who has Medicare in Mercer County

Mercer County is a smaller-city and town county; the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes it as: urban population of 20,000 or more, adjacent to a metro area. Counties like this often sit between a metro area and rural communities, so some residents use hospitals and specialists in a neighboring county and a plan’s network across the county line matters.

CMS counts 16,960 Medicare beneficiaries in Mercer County (2023). Their average age is 71, about the same as the West Virginia average of 72. About 25% are “dual eligible,” meaning they have both Medicare and Medicaid, higher than the state figure of 20%. Where many people have both, special plans for dual-eligible members can matter; see our Medicaid and dual eligible guide, since availability varies by plan and area.

Among people in Original Medicare in Mercer County, CMS reported about 723 emergency room visits and 256 inpatient hospital stays per 1,000 beneficiaries in 2023, which is close to the state average for emergency visits (684) and close to it for hospital stays (250). About 8% received home health care. These figures describe how people use care under Original Medicare; they do not show what any plan covers or costs.

Sources: CMS Medicare Geographic Variation, 2023; USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023.

Common questions

Mercer County Medicare FAQ

Can I get help with Medicare in Mercer County by phone?
Yes. We are an independent agency licensed in West Virginia, and we help Mercer County residents by phone or video call, so you can compare plans from home. There is no pressure to enroll.
Will my Mercer County doctors and hospitals be covered?
On a Medicare Advantage plan it depends on that plan’s network, so before you enroll we check whether your doctors and the hospitals you use, such as Princeton Community Hospital Association, are in network. With a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan you can see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
How many people in Mercer County have Medicare Advantage?
CMS reported 9,608 people enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan out of 16,140 people with Medicare in Mercer County in September 2026, or 59.5%. The West Virginia average was 56.6%.
Do you charge Mercer County residents for help?
No. Our help is free to you. Carriers pay us a commission when we place a policy, and your premium is the same whether you use an agent or enroll on your own.
When can I enroll or change plans?
You can enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday, during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7, or during a Special Enrollment Period if you have a qualifying event such as moving into or within the county.
