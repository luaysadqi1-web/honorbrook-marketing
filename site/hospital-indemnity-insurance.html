Hospital indemnity: cash that wipes out hospital copays

It pays YOU a fixed cash benefit for each day you're admitted — the smartest companion to a Medicare Advantage plan.

The hidden cost of Medicare Advantage hospital stays

Medicare Advantage plans are popular for good reason — many carry a $0 monthly premium and bundle in dental, vision, and drug coverage. But there's a cost most people don't notice until they're lying in a hospital bed: the daily hospital copay. Instead of a single deductible, Advantage plans often charge a per-day copay for the first several days of an inpatient stay, commonly in the range of $300–$450 per day before that copay tops out.

That adds up fast. A short, unplanned admission can leave you owing well over a thousand dollars out of pocket — and that's on top of any other care you receive. It's the kind of surprise bill that turns a stressful hospital stay into a financial one, exactly when you're least able to deal with it.

How hospital indemnity works

A hospital indemnity plan is refreshingly simple. It doesn't try to replace your health insurance — it sits alongside it and writes you a check when you're admitted. Here's what makes it work:

  • It pays a set cash amount per day you're admitted. You choose a daily benefit when you enroll, and the plan pays that fixed amount for each covered day in the hospital.
  • The money is paid directly to you. The benefit goes to you — not the hospital — so there's no waiting on a provider to apply it.
  • You can use it for anything. Hospital copays, the deductible, everyday bills, groceries, a ride home — it's your cash to spend however you need.
  • The monthly premium is affordable. Because the benefit is fixed and predictable, these plans typically cost just a few dollars a month for meaningful protection.
The natural companion: Hospital indemnity isn't a standalone solution — it's the natural companion to a Medicare Advantage plan. Your Advantage plan handles the big medical bills; the indemnity plan hands you cash to wipe out the daily hospital copays that Advantage plans leave on your plate. Together they cover the gap that catches people off guard.

Who it's a great fit for

Hospital indemnity coverage makes the most sense for Medicare Advantage members, who face those per-day inpatient copays directly. If you're on an Advantage plan, a small monthly premium can neutralize a four-figure hospital bill. It's also a smart pick for anyone who simply wants predictable protection — people who would rather pay a known, modest amount each month than gamble on what an unexpected admission might cost. If you value peace of mind and a cash cushion, it's worth a look.

Common questions

Hospital Indemnity FAQ

What is hospital indemnity insurance?
Hospital indemnity insurance is a supplemental plan that pays you a fixed cash benefit for each day you're admitted to the hospital. The money is paid directly to you — not the hospital — so you can use it for copays, bills, groceries, or anything else. It works alongside your existing coverage and is especially useful for offsetting the daily hospital copays a Medicare Advantage plan can charge.
Does it replace my Medicare plan?
No — it's a supplement and companion, not a replacement. Your Medicare Advantage, Medigap, or Original Medicare coverage stays exactly the same. The indemnity plan simply adds a cash benefit that pays you when you're admitted, helping cover the out-of-pocket costs your main plan leaves behind.
How is the benefit paid?
In cash, directly to you — not to the hospital or your doctor. Once you're admitted and file a simple claim, the plan pays a set dollar amount for each day of your covered stay. Because it's your money, you decide how to spend it — on hospital copays, everyday bills, or anything else you need while you recover.
Do I need it with Medigap?
It's less critical with Medigap. A Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan already covers most hospital copays and deductibles, so the gap is smaller. Hospital indemnity is ideal with a Medicare Advantage plan, where daily hospital copays can add up quickly. We'll look at your coverage and tell you honestly whether it makes sense for you.
