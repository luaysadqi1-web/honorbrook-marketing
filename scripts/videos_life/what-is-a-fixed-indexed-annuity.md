On-screen facts and sources:
  - A fixed indexed annuity (FIA) is a deferred annuity issued by an insurance company. Interest is linked to a market index, but the money is not invested in the market.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - Crediting limits: a cap rate (the maximum interest for a period), a participation rate (the percentage of the index gain that counts) and a spread (a percentage subtracted from the gain). They are set by the insurer and can change.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - If the index falls, the credited interest is generally zero rather than negative. This is often called a floor.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - FIAs are designed to be held for a period, commonly several years. Withdrawing more than the free-withdrawal amount during the surrender period can bring a surrender charge. Not a place for emergency money.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - Growth inside a non-qualified FIA is tax-deferred; gains are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - Withdrawals before age 59½ can be subject to an additional 10% federal tax. Confirmed on irs.gov: pension or annuity payments before age 59½ may be subject to an additional 10% tax on early distributions, with exceptions.
    Source: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc410  (page date: IRS Topic no. 410, page last reviewed Oct 16, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - The taxable part of an annuity distribution is treated as ordinary income (confirms the page line on taxable withdrawals).
    Source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p575  (page date: IRS Publication 575 (2025); read Nov 27, 2026)
  - May fit someone near or in retirement who is comfortable leaving the money untouched for the surrender period. Anyone who may need the money soon should be cautious.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - Guarantees depend on the issuing company's claims-paying ability. Insurance and annuity products are not bank deposits and are not FDIC-insured. A CD is FDIC-insured and has fixed terms; each has a different role.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/blog/what-is-a-fixed-indexed-annuity-fia  (page date: page dated Oct 12, 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
  - Principal protection in an FIA is by contract terms, subject to the issuing company's claims-paying ability and to surrender charges if you withdraw early.
    Source: https://honorbrook-insurance.com/annuities  (page date: page updated June 2026; read Nov 27, 2026)
