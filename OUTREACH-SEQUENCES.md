# Honorbrook LinkedIn Outreach Sequences

Drafted 2026-09-05, updated 2026-09-06. Covers the three Gojiberry agents. Voice: measured, plain, no hype —
"The right coverage. The honest way." Every message is allowed to end in "keep what you have."

**Compliance note:** no sequence mentions Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Part D, or plan
names. Cold LinkedIn outreach on Medicare products triggers CMS marketing rules (permission,
Scope of Appointment). Persona 1 (Turning 65) must come from search, referral, and seminar —
never from these agents.

## Agent 1 — Small Employers 1–50 · VA/DC/MD · Benefits

Target: owners and office/practice managers at 1–50 employee firms in construction and home
services, medical/dental practices, real estate, restaurants, salon/medspa, professional
services, childcare, nonprofits.

Opening offer from the strategy doc: *"20-minute benefits feasibility review."*

### Step 1 — Connection request (no note)
Leave the note empty. Note-less requests accept at a higher rate, and there is nothing
worth saying in 300 characters that isn't better said after they accept.

### Step 2 — Day 1, after acceptance

> Thanks for connecting, {{firstName}}.
>
> I work with owners of 2–50 person companies around Northern Virginia on employee benefits —
> usually the ones who've been told their only two options are "offer a group plan" or "offer
> nothing."
>
> There's a third: an ICHRA. You set a monthly allowance, employees buy their own individual
> coverage, it's tax-free to them and deductible to you. No participation minimums.
>
> Not right for everyone. If your team skews older and your current group rates are good,
> staying put usually wins. That's a math question and it's worth actually running.
>
> Happy to run it if it's useful — no cost, and no obligation after.

### Step 3 — Day 3

> One thing worth knowing, {{firstName}}: about half of private-sector employers under 50
> people offer health insurance at all, down from about 62% in 2008. The gap is wider on the
> ancillary side — roughly 39% offer life, 30% short-term disability.
>
> Which means benefits are a live recruiting advantage at your size, not just a cost line.
>
> If you want the 20-minute version: how many W-2 employees, where they live, and what you
> could contribute monthly. That's enough for me to tell you whether group, QSEHRA, or ICHRA
> is the honest answer for you.

### Step 4 — Day 6 (final)

> Last note from me, {{firstName}} — I don't chase.
>
> If benefits come up at your next renewal or your next hire, I'm here. If they don't, no
> harm done and I'm glad to be connected either way.
>
> Luay Sadqi · Honorbrook Insurance, Tysons VA · NPN 21370662

## Agent 2 — Self-Employed Owners · VA/DC/MD

Target: founders, owners, co-founders, presidents of 1–50 person firms — freelancers,
consultants, Realtors, practice owners, contractors, agency owners.

Opening offer from the strategy doc: *"Owner benefits gap review."*

### Step 1 — Connection request (no note)

### Step 2 — Day 1, after acceptance

> Thanks for connecting, {{firstName}}.
>
> I work with self-employed owners around Northern Virginia and DC on the coverage side of
> working for yourself — health insurance without an employer plan, and income protection if
> you can't work.
>
> The second one is the gap I see most. When you're the business, a bad six months isn't a
> sick day, and most owners have no disability coverage at all. About one in four 20-year-olds
> will be out of work for a stretch before retirement age.
>
> No pitch here. If you already have both handled, I'll tell you that and we're done.

### Step 3 — Day 3

> Two things people working for themselves usually don't know, {{firstName}}:
>
> 1. Marketplace savings are based on *estimated* household income for the year, not last
>    year's W-2. Owners whose income moved often qualify for help they assumed they didn't.
> 2. Leaving job-based coverage opens a 60-day special enrollment window — counted from the
>    day coverage ended, not the day you start looking.
>
> If either applies, the timing matters more than the plan choice.

### Step 4 — Day 6 (final)

> Closing the loop, {{firstName}}.
>
> If you ever want a second read on your coverage — or you hire your first W-2 employee and
> the benefits question lands on your desk — I'm easy to find.
>
> Luay Sadqi · Honorbrook Insurance · honorbrook-insurance.com

This is the one persona in the strategy doc that is genuinely institutional B2B, which is
why it suits LinkedIn. The sale is a plan review or an RFP response, not a policy.

### Step 1 — Connection request (no note)

### Step 2 — Day 1, after acceptance

>
> I'm an independent broker in Tysons. A slice of our work is coverage for international
> students and visiting scholars — F-1 and J-1, dependents, OPT participants — and the

>

> requirements, and a plan can otherwise look fine and still fail on them.
>
> If your waiver criteria haven't been reviewed in a couple of cycles, that's usually where
> the gap is.

### Step 4 — Day 6 (final)

> Last one from me, {{firstName}}.
>
> If your office ever puts student health coverage out to bid, or you want a second read on
> waiver criteria before the fall intake, I'd be glad to be on the list. Otherwise, good luck

>
> Luay Sadqi · Honorbrook Insurance, Tysons VA · NPN 21370662
