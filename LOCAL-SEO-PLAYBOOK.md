## Compliance guardrails (read first — these apply to everything below)

These are non-negotiable. Every piece of public content must respect them.

1. **CMS Medicare disclaimer.** Any public content that markets or mentions specific Medicare plans/benefits (Medicare Advantage, Part D, Medigap when promoting plan options) must carry the standard disclaimer:
   > **"We do not offer every plan available in your area. Any information we provide is limited to those plans we do offer in your area. Please contact Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options."**
   Place it in the post, the photo caption, or the linked landing page where the plan content lives.
2. **No implied government affiliation.** Never imply endorsement by Medicare, CMS, the SSA, or "the government." Don't use the Medicare logo, the official Medicare card image, or phrasing like "official Medicare enrollment." You are an independent agency.
3. **No review gating / filtering.** Do **not** route unhappy customers away from Google, screen by star rating before asking, or selectively suppress negative feedback. This violates Google's policy and the FTC's rules on reviews. Ask **everyone** the same way.
4. **No incentives for reviews.** Never offer money, discounts, gift cards, raffle entries, or any other consideration in exchange for a review. Prohibited by Google and the FTC.
5. **No fabricated ratings or stats.** Do not add `aggregateRating` schema, "5-star rated," or any review count until real Google reviews exist. Do not invent statistics anywhere in marketing.
6. **Truthful, verifiable claims only.** "Independent," "licensed in 11 states," and "no cost to you for our services" are fine because they're true. Don't claim "best," "#1," or guaranteed savings.

## 3. Reviews engine

Reviews are the #1 differentiator in Map Pack ranking and click-through. Build a steady, **honest** flow.

### 3.1 Target cadence
- Ask **every** completed client — at the point of greatest satisfaction (right after a plan is finalized / a claim issue is solved / a successful AEP review).
- Aim for **2–4 new Google reviews per month** to start, accelerating during AEP. Steady beats spiky — a sudden burst looks unnatural to Google.
- **Never gate or screen.** Ask all clients the same way regardless of how you think they'll rate. **Never offer anything in return.**

### 3.2 How to ask
- **In person:** "If you've had a good experience, the best way to help us is a quick Google review — I can text you the link right now." Then send it while you're together.
- **Text (SMS):** fastest response; send the short link same-day.
- **Email:** for clients who prefer it or whom you didn't see in person.

### 3.3 Google review short-link
- Get your profile's "Get more reviews" short link from the GBP dashboard (format `https://g.page/r/XXXX/review`), or build the place-ID link `https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=YOUR_PLACE_ID`.
- Shorten/brand it for easy texting, e.g. `honorbrook-insurance.com/review` redirecting to the Google link, or a Bitly. Put it on business cards, email signatures, and the thank-you page.

### 3.4 On-site /reviews page (compliant funnel)
The `/reviews` page should make leaving a Google review **easy** — not filter people.
- Display existing Google reviews (embed/widget) + a prominent **"Leave us a Google review"** button to the short link.
- Provide a **separate, clearly-labeled "Send private feedback"** path (form/email) **shown to everyone alongside** the Google button — never as a gate before it. Anyone can choose either; you never pre-screen by sentiment.
- Do **not** branch users by predicted star rating. Both options are always visible to all visitors.

- If you use a single tracking line on GBP, make sure that exact number is used consistently across all citations too.
